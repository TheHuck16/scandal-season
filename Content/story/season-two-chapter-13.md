### L13.S9 · plot beat · Rose Hartwell, Mr. Trelawney
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — TRELAWNEY. On the counting-house step: how far does she take the frightened banker — into confidence, as a source, or shielded from the trail? (Turns: 3 — the decision itself)*
> He followed her to the outer office — a breach of counting-house protocol, and therefore a confession: he did not want the next words said where the clerks could hear them.
> "Miss Hartwell." On the step, in the Milsom Street air, the iron-grey man looked suddenly older, and suddenly less like an account book. "A word — not as your banker's, but as a man who has daughters. The London houses — Voss and Hallett — they are not merely respectable. They are particular. And particular men, when they learn that a young lady is asking after their traffic, do not send notes. They send collectors. Be careful what you write down, and where you write it."
> The fear in his face was real, and so was the decency underneath — both usable, neither simple. And now the choice, standing on a Bath street in a forgettable dress, with a frightened banker waiting for her answer:
*★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — Trelawney:*
- **Take him fully into confidence** — *Trelawney ally, risk shared.* Rose tells him what she is really tracing — Rackham's scattered paper, the Network's money, the Hartwell mortgages being gathered — and asks him to watch the London traffic for her. He agrees, frightened and game; Bath's banker becomes her watcher inside the web.
