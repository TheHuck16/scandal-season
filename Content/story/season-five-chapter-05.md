**Format key.** [D] dialogue-heavy · [F] fashion-selection (2–3 diegetic options, no coin cost, remembered) · [C] dressing-for-climax ritual (direction + occasion brief, decomposed micro-decisions, story morsels, reveal coda) · [T] texture micro-beat · plot beat (untagged) · Gazette sting · cliffhanger. ★ marks the chapter's exactly 3 key decisions. Animation: Shared throughout — L5 is not a tentpole; the ritual uses the shared ritual presentation.

**Turns.** Every ordinary scene carries 7–10 numbered player turns `(T# · type)` inline: look closer, stance, tone, choice, social maneuver, dialogue, or remembered micro-decision, each closed with a remembered note. Outcomes converge: the game remembers the means (tone, relationship movement, Gazette flavor), never as a plot branch. ★ key-decision scenes carry the decision's 3 options as their turns; the Gazette sting is a single reading turn. The [C] ritual's direction + twenty 5-coin pins are its 21 turns; the ritual runs long and sits outside the 350–450-word scene budget.

**Canon applied:** 1815. The second installment stands at £5,000, assembling on Rose's day, not the dragon's. Lucien de Valcourt never appears in person — only his money and paper act. Lady Agatha Drummond is answered only for now; she returns in S10. Mrs. Nance Bell judges and narrates, soft-voiced, sharp-eyed. The gutter sheet is unnamed. Energy is the only throttle. Iron rule holds: time earns everything money can. No Crown SKU touches the ritual.

**Occasion:** the counter — back in Brighton, the yacht week announced, the Pavilion set afloat; Élise's great commission begun, the atelier at its height. **Preparation ritual:** the announcement gown — to be seen announcing, not asking. **Purse shelf:** the week's small costs, honestly paid; the £5,000, assembling on her day.

**What changes for Rose:** She stops answering the dragon's pressure and starts setting the week's terms — a financial move that puts the £5,000 on her own day, and a social move that makes the yacht week hers to announce. The atelier rises to its height under her backing, and somewhere paper moves that is not hers.
