**Canon applied:** 1815. Rose Hartwell rebuilds Hartwell Park and earns her place; unhurried about marriage, honorable in method. Suitors honorable; routes grow through friendship. Villains sly, never overt. Mrs. Nance Bell is the Gazette's publicly named proprietor from the first edition — no unmasking plot anywhere; the paper judges and narrates, never a mystery, prize, or paywall. Fashion is a courtship mechanic with social consequence. Energy is the only throttle. Iron rule holds: time earns everything money can. Crowns (premium) / coins (soft).

**Occasion:** Quill's chambers in the morning; Élise's atelier at midday; a miniature-painter's studio in the afternoon — three rooms, three kinds of truth. **Preparation ritual:** dressing for the sitting — the studio is the one room in London where no one performs; dress for the woman who sees past the dressing. **Purse shelf:** the sitting's fee, the atelier's account, the cab to the studio.

**What changes for Rose:** She gains Octavia — the sitting names what they are to each other, friendship or romance or neither, by her own naming — and the modiste's ledger teaches her the ton's true economy: who pays, who owes, and what appearances really cost. And Rackham's debts tighten on the Park.

*Purpose: Quill — the codicil explained; what it is, what it changes; Rackham's interest, unspoken and present.*
> "Miss Hartwell." — Quill (rising; a dry man, precise as a ledger, with ink under his nails like a clerk) "The March codicil. Yes. I have been — expecting this inquiry."
> "Since March, Mr. Quill? Or since last night?" — Rose

### L12.S13 · [C] · Rose (alone, dressing)
*Purpose: the sitting ritual — mid-chapter preparation; dressing for the one room in London where no one performs. Direction + occasion brief, ~20 micro-decisions, story morsels between pins, reveal coda.*
> **Occasion brief (published):** "The miniature sitting. The dress code: the painter's studio is the one room in London where no one performs — dress for the woman who sees past the dressing. The miniature flatters simplicity and punishes ornament; the painter has asked for *truth*, and the ton is not invited. Mourning colors forbidden; the sitting lasts two hours, so comfort is strategy. **Editorial criterion: truth.**"
> **The 3 directions:** *The Green* (the simple dark green — the color Octavia once praised across a ballroom: *you look like yourself*) / *The Hartwell* (the Park's palette; the sitter as landowner — sober, rooted, unbowed) / *The Unadorned* (plain to the point of honesty — let the painter find what there is to find).
> **Decomposed steps (~20, 5 coins each, ~100 coins):** dress silhouette → bodice cut → bodice fabric (muslin / cambric / soft merino) → bodice color → sleeve style → sleeve fabric → sleeve color → neckline → skirt style → skirt fabric → skirt color → trim (minimal — the miniature punishes ornament) → shoes → gloves → hair (simple — the painter's eye goes to the face) → hair piece → earrings (owned only) → pendant (owned only) → beauty (the unpainted face — no artifice the painter didn't ask for) → shawl → finishing accessory.
> **Story morsels (between pins):** Élise on who else sits for miniatures ("Cecilia Vane asked after Mrs. Wren's *rates*, miss — last week"); the ledger's margins, remembered — *Vane pays coin, on the nail*; Octavia's note, delivered mid-pinning: *the light is north. Come as you are. — O.*
