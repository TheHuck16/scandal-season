**Canon applied:** 1815. Rose Hartwell rebuilds Hartwell Park and earns her place; unhurried about marriage, honorable in method. Suitors honorable; routes grow through friendship. Villains sly, never overt. Mrs. Nance Bell is the Gazette's publicly named proprietor from the first edition — no unmasking plot anywhere; the paper judges and narrates, never a mystery, prize, or paywall. Fashion is a courtship mechanic with social consequence. Energy is the only throttle. Iron rule holds: time earns everything money can. Crowns (premium) / coins (soft).

**Occasion:** The morning after Drummond's ball — the drawing room will fill with callers, the Gazette's verdict is already in the coffee-houses, and Quill's chambers wait. **Preparation ritual:** the day-after dressing — look rested, unruffled, un-accused; nothing that shines too hard, nothing that apologizes. **Purse shelf:** the morning's coins — the messenger to Quill, the chairmen's tips, the crossing-sweeper's smile.

**What changes for Rose:** She survives the ball's aftermath on her own feet — the Gazette's verdict answered or endured on her terms, three notes weighed and one answered, and Quill's chambers secured for tomorrow: the March codicil, at last, within reach.
