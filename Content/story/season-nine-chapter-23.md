### L23.S2 · plot beat · Rose (alone) — "How to Face the Ton"
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — HOW TO FACE THE TON (attend everything, attend nothing, or attend once). The facing, decided. (Turns: 3 — the decision)*
> *(The library in the morning: the week's first question, and it won't wait for the second cup of tea. She lays the invitations in three rows on the desk and prices the roads the way she prices everything now — by cost and by what they buy. Attend everything: be seen in every room, every tea, every evening, the defendant's face worn openly — nerve as the argument, the town reading the nerve all week. Attend nothing: the house her fortress, the shutters of her own making; the town tries the case without the defendant and writes its own account of her absence. Attend once: one evening, the one that matters — stake the whole week on a single night and let the one appearance be the whole statement. Each road meets the ton. Each meets it differently.)*
*★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — How to Face the Ton:*
- **Attend everything** — *show up everywhere, all week, and let nerve be the argument.* She goes everywhere — every room, every tea, every evening, the face worn openly. (Consequence: the town reads the nerve all week — but going everywhere is going exposed, and any room can price an exposed woman.)
- **Attend nothing** — *stay home behind her own shutters; let dignity be the argument.* She goes nowhere — the house her fortress, the trial tried in her absence. (Consequence: total dignity, total absence — but silence lets the town answer for her.)
- **Attend once** — *one evening, the one that matters; stake the whole week on it.* She goes once — the one evening that matters, the week's whole argument. (Consequence: the appearance is the statement — but one night is a gamble, and it stakes the week.)
> *(She makes the choice, and the game keeps the manner of it — everywhere, nowhere, or once. The ton was always going to try the week; the part that is hers is how she walks through it.)*

### L23.S10 · plot beat · Rose (alone) — "Hugh's Standing"
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 2/3 — HUGH'S STANDING (defend him, abandon him, or stand apart). The decision made. (Turns: 3 — the decision)*
> *(The library, evening: the week's second question, and the harder one. Hugh Hartwell moves through the town's trial the way he moves through everything — thirty, courteous, pitying — and the town is weighing him alongside her. She prices the roads. Defend him: speak for the cousin in the drawing rooms, name the righteous man righteous in front of the town, and let the town read the generosity however it reads it. Abandon him: leave him to the town's weighing unchampioned, the courtesy she was shown returned with silence, and let the town answer for her. Stand apart: his standing the town's business, hers the hearing's — step outside the question and let both verdicts fall where they fall. His father's money kept the Park standing; the money is owed as a debt, not the house. That is the law's column. The town's column is still being written. Each road places her. Each places her differently.)*
