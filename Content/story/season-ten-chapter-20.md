### L20.S1 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the years' turning — the Englishman's years; Laurent, the unmasked. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The boots are the first thing Rose notices — Laurent's boots, by the boot-room door, muddied to the ankle at an hour when no French comte has any business being muddy. It is barely light. The house is still holding its breath. And the boots say, plain as print, that the Englishman has been out in the Park since dawn, doing whatever it is he does out there before the household book.*
> *(Hodges, passing with the lamps, follows her look. "The Comte walks, miss," he says, with the particular gravity of a man reporting a change in the weather. "The parkland. Every morning. He has taken to naming the trees." A pause. "In English."*
> *"Does he get them right?"*
> *"Increasingly, miss. The lime took some weeks." Hodges picks up a lamp, considers the boots, and adds — because Hodges, too, has been watching the years turn: "He cleans them himself, miss. Wouldn't let the boy do it. Said the mud was —" the butler searches for the word — "'earned.'"*
> *(Earned. Rose stands in the boot room with the lamps going out one by one, and thinks about the turning of Laurent's years — not as an event, with trumpets, but as a pair of boots. The Comte de Varenne, thirty-one, French to the bone, has chosen England: the Park, the rain, the mud. And the choosing, once done, turns out to be a daily practice — like the household book, like the chocolate, like the naming of trees in a language he is still learning.*
> *(It began without announcement, as the true things do. Not with the formal ask — that came at the masquerade, the mask off for good, the old world given one more night and then set aside — but with the small surrenders after: the polish laid down, then the management, then the beautiful survival itself. What was left was a man speaking directly, the way she does. It terrified them both, at first. Then it became the ground.*
> *(The ground: England. Not Paris, not the brilliant match his family had pressed for, not the de Varenne name restored the way they wanted it restored. England — the mud, the mist, the directness. She looks at the boots a moment longer, and understands that the unmasking was not an evening's work. It was an arrival. The mask, removed, has stayed off — and the face underneath keeps choosing, every morning, before breakfast.*
> *(She leaves the boots where they are — earned mud, not to be disturbed — and goes to order the chocolate, both cups, because some liturgies continue even when the Englishman is unmasked. Especially then.*

### L20.S2 · [D] · Rose, Laurent
*Purpose: Laurent, the years' — the Englishman, the unmasked; the direct, the kept. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The morning room, mid-morning, and Laurent is at the writing desk with three letters spread before him like a losing hand — writing, crossing out, writing again. He looks up when Rose comes in, and does the thing the unmasked do now: he does not manage his expression. He lets her see the difficulty.*
> *"The letter to my family," he says. "I have written it in French four times. It is perfect, and it is a lie." He pushes the French drafts aside. "So I am writing it in English. It will be clumsy, and it will be true."*
> *(Rose sits. The household book stays closed. This is not a morning for arithmetic.*
> *"Read me the true one," she says.*
> *"'My dear mother —'" he begins, and stops. "No. In English it must be simpler. 'Mother. I have chosen England. I have chosen her. The de Varenne name will not be restored the way you hoped. It will be —'" he searches — "kept. Differently." He looks up. "Is 'differently' too cruel?"*
> *"It's honest. Cruel would be pretending."*
> *"The French instinct," he says, "is to survive beautifully and fight later. My family survived the ruin that way — the speculation took everything, and we survived it beautifully. It was magnificent. It was also exhausting." He meets her eyes. "I am tired of being magnificent, Rose. I would like to try being kept."*
> *(She comes around the desk, and reads over his shoulder, and corrects one word — not the grammar, the courage — and he takes the correction the way he takes all her corrections now: as a form of love.*
> *"'Kept, differently,'" he reads back, trying it. "Yes. That is the whole of it." He signs it — the careful English hand, still learning — and sands it, and folds it. "The brilliant match they wanted for me —" a small, unmasked smile — "will have to find another comte. This one is taken. In English."*
> *(He seals the letter, and the wax is the Park's own red, and Rose understands that the directness is not a talent. It is a discipline — practiced at a writing desk, in a morning room, one true sentence at a time. The letter goes out with the post. The family will read it in Paris and understand, or not. The choosing, freely made, by both, does not require their permission. That was the whole point.*
> *("Kept, differently," Rose says, trying it herself, and finds it will do. Some sentences are load-bearing. This is one of them.*

### L20.S3 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the mornings — Laurent's; the unmasked's, the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The estate office, and Laurent is frowning at the home farm account book the way other men frown at battle maps — which, Rose reflects, is exactly what it is to him. A field of engagement. The enemy: English weights and measures.*
> *"Explain to me again," he says, without looking up, "the stone."*
> *"Fourteen pounds."*
> *"Fourteen pounds is a stone. Sixteen ounces is a pound. Eight stone is —" he counts on his fingers, in French, then catches himself — "a hundredweight, which is not a hundred of anything." He looks up, betrayed. "Your country measures things the way your aunt plays piquet. By private agreement."*
> *"The bushel?"*
> *"The bushel I have mastered. The bushel is honest. It is the hundredweight that is French in its deception." He returns to the book, brow furrowed, actually reading — not managing the reading, the way the mask used to manage everything, but doing it: the direct brow, the unperformed attention.*
> *(Rose, bringing the household book out of habit, watches him work through the column — the seed, the feed, the labor, all in stones and bushels and hundredweights — and understands what the morning actually contains. Not a lesson in arithmetic. A man, inhabiting the ground he chose, down to its absurd measurements. The mask would have had a steward for this. The face does it himself, badly at first, then all at once well.*
> *"The lower field," Laurent announces, triumphant, "requires —" he checks the figure twice — "requires drainage, and I can prove it in hundredweights." He sits back. "In Paris, I survived beautifully. In England, I do sums. It is less elegant." His look doesn't waver. "It is more true. I have decided to prefer it."*
> *"The drains?"*
> *"The drains are insufficient, and I can now prove it in two systems of measurement." He closes the book with satisfaction. "This is what choosing England means, Rose. Not the terrace. The hundredweight."*
> *(She laughs — and the estate office holds the sound, dust and sealing wax and all — and thinks about the small surrenders, one after another, that the unmasking was made of. The polish. The management. The beautiful survival. And now the hundredweight, surrendered to. The choosing, practiced daily, in stones and bushels.*
> *(At nine the papers are set aside, and the morning goes on around them both — the house, the Park, the absurd honest measures — and Rose writes nothing in the margin, because some mornings are the margin.*

### L20.S4 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the mask — the removed; the years', the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(Augusta finds it in the library — the library drawer, the one that sticks in damp weather — and brings it to the morning room at tea-time with the air of a woman producing evidence: a domino mask, black silk, the elastic gone slack with years. The masquerade's. His.*
> *"It was in with the estate papers," Augusta says. "Filed. Your Frenchman files his masks with the drainage reports. I thought you should see it before I burned it."*
> *(Rose takes it. The silk is soft, the shape still holding the curve of a face. She remembers the night — the last great masquerade, the old world given one more night — and the moment in it: the mask, taken off, for good. Not lifted for the evening. Removed. The choice, made freely, this time by both, with everyone watching.*
> *"Don't burn it," she says. "Not yet. Let him see it first."*
> *"Sentiment," Augusta says, "is the enemy of bonfires. But very well." She pours the tea. "The mask kept a ruined family elegant through the worst of it, child. It kept your Frenchman safe — or safe-looking — for years. It deserves better than my grate. It deserves —" the dry pause — "a ceremony."*
> *(That is how it is decided: the mask will be burned, but properly — in the kitchen garden, at dusk, with the household as witnesses. Augusta, who never does anything by halves, sends Hodges for the brazier. Letitia, hearing of it, declares it "the first sensible bonfire of the Season" and offers to bring the negus. The servants' hall is informed. The mask, filed for years with the drainage reports, is about to be retired with honors.*

The mask was never a lie, Laurent will tell her later. It was a postponement. Beautifully managed. She turns it over in her hands, and understands the scale of what he laid down. Not a habit. > *"Tonight," she tells it, "you retire." The mask says nothing, having said everything it had to say years ago. She sets it by the book, and goes down to tell Laurent that Augusta has planned his mask a funeral, and finds she is smiling — because only this house would give a retirement ceremony to a piece of silk, and only this man would deserve it.)*

### L20.S5 · [D] · Rose, Laurent
*Purpose: Laurent, the mask's — the Englishman, the removed; the truth, the kept. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The kitchen garden at dusk, and the brazier is lit — Augusta presiding with the gravity of a woman conducting a state funeral, Letitia with the negus, Hodges with the household assembled at a respectful distance, and Laurent holding the domino mask in both hands like a man holding a sleeping animal.*
> *"Say something," Augusta instructs. "One doesn't burn a career without remarks."*
> *(Laurent looks at the mask — the black silk, the slack elastic — and his look is there, unmasked, in the firelight.*
> *"It was never a lie," he says. "I want that kept. It was a postponement. The French instinct: survive beautifully, fight later. My family survived the ruin that way — the speculation took everything, and we survived it beautifully. It was magnificent. It was also exhausting." He looks at Rose. "The mask was for the things too dangerous for faces. There were more of them than you'd think. A ruined name. A family's expectations. Paris."*
> *(The garden holds its breath. The brazier ticks.*
> *"And the removal?" Augusta asks — dry, precise, entitled to ask. "The unmasking — what was it, if the mask wasn't a lie?"*
> *"Permission." His look does not waver. "She gave me permission, in English, to stop postponing. The masquerade — the mask off for good. England, chosen. The choosing, freely made, by both." A pause. "The face was always there. It merely lacked the English to express itself."*
> *(He lays the mask on the brazier. The silk catches — quickly, beautifully, the way it did everything — and the household watches it go, and Letitia, of all people, wipes her eyes and claims it is the smoke.*
> *"To the postponed," Augusta says, raising her negus, "arrived at last." They drink. The elastic curls in the heat. And Rose understands that the removed's truth is not dramatic. It is arithmetic, of a kind: years, postponed; now, kept. The mask, retired with honors. The face, resident.)*
> *(Afterward, walking back through the dark garden, Laurent takes her hand — the unperformed gesture — and says, very quietly: "It is less beautiful than the survival. It is more true. I have decided to prefer it." The brazier cools behind them. The ceremony is over. The retirement, unlike the postponement, is permanent.*

### L20.S6 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the evenings — Laurent's; the unmasked's, the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *"The drains," Laurent intones, one hand raised like a man addressing the National Assembly, "are insufficient."*
> *(The drawing room after dinner. The rain doing its soft persistent work on the glass. Laurent with the steward's report, feet up, reading the lower field's drainage in the accents of French tragedy — which makes Augusta's needle pause, which makes Rose choke on her tea, which makes Letitia applaud from the card table.*
> *"Laurent," Augusta says, without looking up, "if you do the drains again I shall retire."*
> *"You love the drains. They are the highlight of your evening." He finds the page. "The culvert —" a significant pause — "the culvert is the tragedian's great soliloquy. 'O culvert, culvert —'"*
> *"No," says the whole room, variously.*
> *"'O culvert,'" Laurent continues, undeterred, "'thou obstructed artery of the lower field —'"*
> *(Rose watches him — the direct eyes bright over the papers, the absolute delight in the performance — and thinks about what the evening actually contains. Nothing that would fill a paragraph of the Gazette. A report, a tragedian, the drains. And yet the evening sits in her like a good meal. This is the unmasking, she understands: the charm transfigured. The man who survived beautifully now performs the drainage report with the same total commitment and twice the truth.*
> *"Read the bit about the sluice," Letitia calls. "The sluice is my favorite."*
> *"The sluice," Laurent agrees, "is underappreciated." He clears his throat. "'O sluice —'"*
> *(Augusta retires — not really, she only threatens it — and the evening goes on: the rain, the fire, the tragedian's voice doing culverts beautifully. Later Rose will try to describe it and fail, because there is nothing to describe. It was the kind of evening the house had been building toward without knowing it.*
> *(Upstairs, much later, she listens to the house settle — the particular quiet of an evening that asked nothing and gave everything — and laughs, quietly, into the dark. The mask would have been magnificent tonight. The face was better. The tragedian does culverts beautifully, and means it. The house, she thinks, has never once been better entertained in all its years.*

### L20.S7 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: England — the chosen; the years', the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(Beating the bounds — the old English custom, the parish walking its boundaries once a year so nobody forgets where the ground ends — and this year the steward has invited the Comte, because the Comte asked. Laurent, at the churchyard gate with a willow wand like everyone else, looks entirely serious about the whole affair.*
> *"Explain to me again," he says to Rose, falling into step, "why we are hitting the boundary stones."*
> *"So the parish remembers them. The boys get bumped on the stones, traditionally, so they remember."*
> *"And the adults?"*
> *"The adults get luncheon."*
> *(He considers this fair. They walk — the whole parish strung out along the hedgerows, the boys already jostling for position at the first stone — and Laurent watches it all with the attention he once gave to Paris salons: the steward calling the marks, the old men arguing about a ditch, the willow wands tapping the stones.*
> *(At the third stone — the one by the lower field, the insufficiently drained one — the steward turns to him. "Your land now, in a manner of speaking, Comte. You do the honors." And Laurent steps forward, and taps the stone with his willow wand, and the boys cheer, and someone bumps young Pike's boy on it for tradition, and Laurent laughs — the unmasked laugh, entirely his.*
> *"The ground," he says to Rose, walking on, "has edges. In Paris, the ground was —" he searches — "negotiable. Here it is marked. With stones. That one can hit." He holds her gaze. "I have chosen a country that hits its boundaries with sticks so as not to forget them. It is absurd."*
> *"It is England."*
> *"It is England," he agrees, "and I am beating its bounds with a willow wand, and I have never been more serious about anything." He taps the next stone with particular ceremony. "This one is mine. I have decided."*
> *(They walk the whole parish — the stones, the ditches, the luncheon — and Rose watches him learn the ground the way other men learn a language: stone by stone, with ceremony. What England asks of him, daily: the directness. The drains. The English words for trees. What England gives him: the ground, the rain, the woman who corrects his pronunciation. It seems, suddenly, like the fairest trade anyone ever made.*
> *(At the last stone he stops, and looks back over the walked parish — and does something the masked man never did: nothing. Just the look, direct, taking it in. "It is absurd," he says, "how English it is." "It is England," Rose says. "Absurdity is included."*

### L20.S8 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the years — Laurent's; the unmasked's, the turned. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The tailor has come up from the town — the morning room turned fitting-room, the light good, the mirror brought in — and Laurent is standing on the little dais in his shirtsleeves while the old French coats are held up one by one for judgment. Ten years of Paris tailoring. Beautiful. Managed. Doomed.*

> *"And this one?" Laurent indicates a bottle-green masterpiece with too many buttons.*

> *(Rose, from the window seat with the household book unopened on her lap, watches the years turn in cloth. Each coat, held up: the beautiful survival, examined. Some can be altered — taken in, shortened, made English eventually. Some cannot be unmade. The tailor, without knowing it, is conducting the unmasking's final audit.*
> *"The claret," Laurent decides. "Alter the claret. The green stays as it is." He looks at Rose in the mirror. "The face, English. The coat, on evenings, French. That is the settlement. The mask retired; the wardrobe, bilingual."*
> *(The tailor chalks, pins, mutters in the particular dialect of his trade. Augusta, passing the door, stops — watches a moment — and says, dry as dust: "The green was always too many buttons. > *"The years," he says, as the tailor pins the claret's shoulder, "turn in cloth before they turn in men. I have been the performance for years. Now I am the practice." The masked, become something rarer: the man the truth was for. She writes nothing in the margin. The tailor's chalk is keeping the record.*
> *("English, eventually," the tailor says, standing back from the claret. Laurent looks at himself in the mirror — the pinned shoulder, his steady look — and nods. Eventually is the English word he has learned best. It suits him.*

### L20.S12 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the Assembly — Laurent's; the convened, the Englishman's. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The carriage to the Assembly, and Laurent is nervous — actually nervous, the unmasked kind, which is worse than the masked kind because there is nowhere to hide it. He keeps adjusting his cuffs. Augusta, opposite, watches with the needle-sharp attention she gives everything.*
> *"You will waltz," Augusta says. It is not a question.*
> *"I will waltz," Laurent agrees. "I am practicing England. The waltz is" — he searches — "the examination."*
