### L25.S1 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the convened's turning — the ton, the gathered; the future, the years. (Turns: 7)*
> *(Dawn, and the ballroom is empty. Rose stands in the doorway with a candle, and the room stands back at her — the floor waxed to a dark shine, the chairs ranked along the walls like a parliament of the patient, the chandeliers hung with their candles unlit, waiting. It smells of beeswax and last night's nothing. No one has danced here yet today. No one has done anything here yet today, except the footmen at six, and they are gone.)*
> *(She walks the length of it, the candle throwing her shadow long across the boards. Ten seasons of scaffolding, and now this: a room that exists for one purpose, and the purpose is tonight. The Hartwell Assembly — her assembly, the one she named in the winter and convened on her terms — gathers here in twelve hours. The ton is coming to Hartwell Park — here, not London, not Almack's.)*
> *(She stops at the far end, by the windows that give onto the terrace. The Park is still dark beyond the glass, the lake a flat black coin. Somewhere out there the tenants are waking, the farms starting, the canal money doing its quiet work. The estate is a working estate — she has said it so often it ought to be dull by now, and it is not dull. The ballroom is the flower. The farms are the root.)*
> *(The candle gutters. She shields it with her hand, and the wax is warm against her palm, and she thinks of the first time she stood in a ballroom and understood that the room was a machine for being looked at. She was seventeen. She is twenty-five now, and the machine is hers, and the looking will be done on her terms. That is the difference between then and now, and it is not a small difference.)*
> *(She turns back toward the door. The household will be stirring. The day — the first day of the convened, the gathered, the turned — is about to begin, and the ballroom will fill, and the candles will be lit, and the ton will come up the drive. But for now it is empty, and it is hers, and the empty is a kind of promise.)*

### L25.S2 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the gathering — the ton's; the Assembly's, the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The library, mid-morning, and the guest list is spread across the desk like a map of a country she is about to invade. Rose reads it with a pencil in her hand, and the pencil has not moved in twenty minutes, because there is nothing to correct. The names are all here. The ton, the gathered — the whole glittering roll of them, from the duchesses down to the ambitious baronets' wives who will tell everyone they were asked first.)*
> *(She knows what each name costs. Not in money — the money is spent, the accounts are closed, the Assembly is funded — but in nerve. Each name is a small negotiation she has already won: the woman who cut her years ago and must now smile; the man who called Hartwell a ruin and must now admire the plaster; the girl who will watch her all evening to learn how it is done. They are coming because she convened them. She did not ask to be let in. She sent the invitations.)*
> *(The list is in her own hand — she wrote it herself, all four dozen names, because Augusta taught her that a hostess who does not know her own list is a guest at her own party. Augusta is seventy-two and has forgotten nothing since the Regency began. Rose is twenty-five and intends to forget nothing either, starting with this: that the list is not a roster but a grammar. Who sits near whom. Who is kept waiting. Who is introduced to whom, and in what order. The ton reads these things the way Harrow reads charters — for what is not said.)*
> *(She sets the pencil down. The list does not need her. It needed her in February, when she drew it up; it needed her in March, when she revised it; it needs her now only to be executed, and the household will execute it. That is what the ten seasons were for — not just the plaster and the portraits, but this: a machine that runs, and a woman who built it, and a day when she can stand in the library and look at the map and know the country is hers.)*
> *(Outside the window, a carriage turns into the drive — the first of the early arrivals, the ones who come for the house party before the ball. She does not go to the window. There will be time. The list holds. The gathering holds.)*

### L25.S3 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the gathered — the convened's; the years', the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(Noon, and Rose walks the gallery — the long west gallery, the portraits hung straight, the light falling in slabs across the floor. The company is coming. Not the ball yet — the ball is tonight — but the house party, the annual's, the kept: the guests arriving for the days around the ball, the dinners, the mornings, the whole apparatus of a country house gathering. They are on the roads now, in carriages, with their maids and their boxes and their expectations.)*
> *(She thinks of them in the aggregate, the way a general thinks of an army: not as faces but as forces. The old guard, who remember when Hartwell was a scandal and have decided to forget. The new money, who want to be seen seeing it. The young, who have heard the stories and want the reality. The curious, who are the most dangerous and the most useful, because the curious talk, and talk is the Assembly's currency.)*
> *(Letitia would have a joke for this moment — something about armies and guest lists, something that would make the whole thing bearable. Letitia is in London, or on the road; the letters said Tuesday. Rose does the arithmetic the way she always does when she misses her: what would Letitia say? Probably that a hostess is just a general who has to smile while the battle happens. Probably that the punch will run out. Letitia is funny without trying, which is the only kind of funny that survives a house party.)*
> *(The portraits watch her walk — the Ravencrofts, the Hartwells, the whole long argument of her blood, framed and dusted. The Hartwells were builders before they were anything else. The building is the blood.)*
> *(At the gallery's end she stops. The drive is visible from here, and there are carriages on it now — two, three — the company's first wave. She watches them come, small and dark against the green, and the gathering is no longer a list but a fact, and the fact is hers.)*

### L25.S4 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the room — the Assembly's; the whole's, the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The ballroom again, at two o'clock, and it is not empty anymore — not of people, but of emptiness. The footmen have been and gone; the chairs are placed; the candles are in the chandeliers, unlit, like a held breath. Rose stands in the middle of the floor and turns slowly, taking the room's measure the way she took the gallery's this morning: as inventory, as instrument, as hers.)*
> *(It is the Assembly's room. That is what she decided in the winter, when she named it — not London's room, not the ton's room, but the Assembly's, which is to say Hartwell's, which is to say hers. The ton has rooms. Almack's has a room. Every great house in Mayfair has a room. This one is different, and the difference is the point: they are coming to her ground. The floor they will dance on is the floor she paid to wax. The candles they will be lit by are the candles she chose.)*
> *(She thinks of the first ball she ever attended — seventeen, borrowed gloves, the terror of the doorway. She thinks of the balls since: the watching, the waiting, the learning. > *(The light through the tall windows is white and straight. In twelve hours it will be candlelight, and the room will be gold, and the music will start, and the whole machine will move. > *(The afternoon light slants through the tall windows — the dust turning, the floor gleaming. She walks to the center and stands where the first dance will begin.)*
> *(The room is the Assembly's — not London's, not the ton's. The distinction matters: they come to her ground, and the ground is the law.)*
> *(She leaves by the terrace door, into the white afternoon. The room holds its breath behind her.)*

### L25.S5 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the future — the convened's; the coming, the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(Late afternoon, and Rose is at the bedroom window — the one that looks west, over the Park to the horizon. The future is out there, though it does not look like much: fields, the lake's silver edge, the cottages with their chimneys, the road to London a pale line. The convened's future. The coming, the kept.)*
> *(She has spent ten seasons building toward a horizon she could not see. That is the truth of it, and it is not a complaint. The restoration was always a bet — that the house could be saved, that the money would hold, that she could learn what she needed faster than the ruin could teach her otherwise. The bet is won. The house stands. And now the horizon is not a question but an answer: the Assembly, annual, hers, the ton convened on her terms for as long as she chooses to convene it.)*
> *(The charter is in the desk behind her — the Assembly's charter, the one she drafted with Quill's help and Harrow's archive at her elbow. It says what the Assembly is: a yearly house party and ball at Hartwell Park, convened by the mistress of Hartwell, on her terms. It does not say what the Assembly is for. > *(A carriage crosses the distant road — someone arriving, or someone leaving, or someone merely passing. The Park does not care. The Park was here before the ton and will be here after. > *(She turns from the window. The future is coming — the carriages, the candles, the ball. But for now it is late afternoon, and the horizon is quiet, and it is hers.)*

### L25.S7 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the terms — the convened's; the Hartwell's, the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The study, after dinner — though she has not dined; the guests are dining without her, or rather before her, the house party's long table filling while she steals this hour. Rose sits at the desk with the Assembly's charter open before her, and the terms are there in Quill's clean clerkly hand: the Hartwell Assembly, convened annually by the mistress of Hartwell Park, on her terms. The terms, the convened's. The Hartwell's, the kept.)*
> *(She reads them the way she always reads legal paper — once for the words, once for the trapdoors. There are no trapdoors. She wrote it that way. The terms are simple, because simple terms are the hardest to argue with: she convenes; they come; the house is hers; the rules are hers. Anyone who does not like the terms is free not to come, and everyone is coming.)*
> *(It is the law of the Assembly, and it is her law, and she feels the weight of it the way she felt the weight of the first mortgage paper she ever signed — the particular gravity of putting your name to something that will outlast your mood. The charter will be here next year, and the year after, whether she is tired or not, whether the ton is kind or not. That is what law is for: to hold the shape when the will wavers.)*
> *(Harrow would approve of the charter's plainness — he is sixty-one and has spent his life in archives, and he trusts a document that says what it means. She remembers him peering at the draft through his spectacles, nodding once, saying nothing, which from Harrow is a speech. The archive keeps the charter's copy. The house keeps the original. She keeps the meaning.)*
> *(She closes the charter. The terms are set. The convened's law is the Hartwell's law, and the Hartwell's law is hers, and tonight the ton will gather under it — not knowing, most of them, that they are under anything at all. That is the best kind of law: the kind that feels like hospitality.)*

### L25.S9 · ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — THE CONVENED'S LAW (what the gathered's law is: the gathering, the terms, or the keeping). The years deepen; decide the law. (Turns: 3 — the decision)*
> *(The charter is closed, the calendar is marked, the ballroom is ready — and there remains the question underneath all of it, the one she has been circling since winter. The convened asks its law. Not the charter's law, which is written, but the deeper law: what is the gathering FOR? What does the Assembly mean, when the candles are lit and the ton is in the room?)*
> *(She stands at the study window — the Park dark beyond, the house humming below — and turns the three answers over the way she turns a coin, looking for the true face. The gathering: that the law is the Assembly itself, the ton convened, the room full — that what matters is that they came, and keep coming, and the coming is the thing. The terms: that the law is hers — the Hartwell's terms, the convened's terms — that what matters is that she sets them, and holds them, and the holding is the power. The keeping: that the law is what is held — the gathered, the kept, the future — that what matters is not the night but what the night preserves.)*
> *(They are all true. That is the difficulty. The gathering is true — without the ton, the Assembly is an empty room. The terms are true — without her law, the Assembly is just another ball. The keeping is true — without the future, the Assembly is a single night, and single nights fade. She has built all three, over ten seasons, and now she must name which one is the law — which one the convened will remember, when the histories are written and the Gazette has had its say.)*
> *(She thinks of Harrow's archive — the charters, the plain sentences, the law laid down in ink. A law is a choice about what matters most. The archive does not record everything; it records what the law says to record. So with the convened: she must choose what it keeps, and the choosing will shape what it becomes.)*
> *(The house is quiet. The decision is hers alone — no counsel, no committee, the convener's prerogative and the convener's burden. She takes up the pen, and the ink is black, and the page is white, and the years deepen around her like water around a stone.)*
*★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — The convened's law:*
- **The gathering** — *the Assembly, lawed.* The law is the gathering — the Assembly itself, the ton convened, the room full: what matters is that they came, and keep coming. (Consequence: the law will be the coming — the room full, the ton gathered, year on year.)
- **The terms** — *the Hartwell, lawed.* The law is the terms — hers, the Hartwell's: what matters is that she sets them, and holds them, and the holding is the power. (Consequence: the law will be her terms — set by her hand, held by her will.)
- **The keeping** — *the held, lawed.* The law is keeping — what the night preserves: not the night itself, but what the night keeps. (Consequence: the law will be the preserving — the gathered kept, the future held.)
> *(The game remembers the choice — and the means: gathering, terms, or keeping. The convened's law is hers.)*
