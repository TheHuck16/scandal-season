**STATUS: FULL** — September 27, 2026.

**Sources:** `story/season-one-scenes.md` (Chapter 5 inventory — 9 named beats expanded to the 40-scene budget), `story/season-one-chapters-1-3-pilot.md` (format), `story/book-one-arc.md` (Season One engine: "The Codicil"), `content/chapter-dressing.md` (locked ritual rules), `content/characters.json` (canon names).

**Format key.** [D] dialogue-heavy · [F] fashion-selection (2–3 diegetic options, no coin cost, remembered) · [C] dressing-for-climax ritual (direction + occasion brief, decomposed micro-decisions, story morsels, reveal coda) · [T] texture micro-beat · plot beat (untagged) · Gazette sting · cliffhanger. ★ marks the chapter's exactly 3 key decisions. Animation: Shared throughout — L5 is not a tentpole; the ritual uses the shared ritual presentation.

**Canon applied:** 1815. Rose Hartwell rebuilds Hartwell Park and earns her place; unhurried about marriage, honorable in method. Suitors honorable; villains sly, never overt. Mrs. Nance Bell is the Gazette's publicly named proprietor from the first edition — no unmasking plot anywhere; the paper judges and narrates, never a mystery, prize, or paywall. Fashion is a courtship mechanic with social consequence. Energy is the only throttle. Iron rule holds: time earns everything money can. Crowns (premium) / coins (soft). No Crown SKU touches the ritual.

**Occasion:** Hartwell Park itself — the mistress returns. **Preparation ritual:** the country gown — authority without display. **Purse shelf:** the journey's costs; the estate's arithmetic.

**What changes for Rose:** She sees the ruin up close for the first time — the empty rectangle, the ledgers, the steward's fear, Rackham's claim in writing — and the Park stops being a miniature on a mantel and becomes a battlefield she has walked; the vow is made where the Gainsborough hung, and Rackham has walked the gallery before her.

*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 2/3 — THE LEDGERS (inventory decision 2). The figures, faced — or not.*

### L5.S11 · plot beat · Rose, Letitia
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — THE EMPTY RECTANGLE (inventory decision 1). What the absence becomes.*
*★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — The empty rectangle:*
- **Vow to fill it** — *Estate motive set, hard.* "It will hang again — or its equal. I swear it on this wall." *(The vow, aloud; Letitia hears it.)*
- **Grieve it** — *Letitia comforts; the loss, allowed.* She lets the grief come — for the painting, for her father, for everything sold — and does not apologize for it.
- **Commit it to memory as evidence** — *Information+.* Detail by detail — the nail holes, the fading, the measurements — the absence itself becomes testimony.

### L5.S16 · [D] · Rose, the mason's boy
*Purpose: the boy's testimony — what the gentleman asked; where he walked.*
> "What did he ask, exactly?" — Rose

> "Did he pay you, for your answers?" — Rose

### L5.S17 · [D] · Rose, Mr. Hodges
*Purpose: the steward's fear — he's been paid to be afraid; the money, the threats, the shame. (inventory beat)*
> "Mr. Hodges." — Rose (in the steward's room, the door shut) "Has anyone paid you — to be afraid?"
> *(The silence is the answer. Then:)* "Fifty pounds, miss. A year. For — for *noticing* things. Who comes down, what the mistress does." — Hodges (his face in his hands) "And if I stopped — men came. Not gentlemen. *Men*. They walked the gallery, miss. Like they owned it."
> "You will take no more of it." — Rose (her hand on his shoulder) "Whatever they threatened, the Park protects its own now. That is my word — and a Hartwell's word is the one thing Rackham has not managed to buy."
