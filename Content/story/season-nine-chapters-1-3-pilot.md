### L1.S8 · plot beat · Rose (alone) — "How to Receive It"
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — THE RECEIVING (how to receive the claim: read it alone, read it with Quill, or read it with Augusta). The claim is received; decide before the claim decides for her. (Turns: 3 — the decision)*
> *(The library, evening: the letter read, the claim's shape understood, the cousin named. The question now is not what the claim says but how she receives it — and the receiving, Rose knows, is half the battle. She walks the carpet's length and prices the three roads: alone, with Quill, with Augusta.)*
*★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — The Receiving:*
- **Read it alone** — *the night's quiet, no witness.* She reads it again in the library's stillness — no witness to the first flinch, her own counsel first. (Consequence: the flinch stays hers alone — unwitnessed, unhurried; but counsel delayed is counsel weakened, and the night is long.)
- **Take it to Quill** — *the solicitor's reading, the professional.* She carries it back to the chambers in the morning — the law's first word, the professional's eye. (Consequence: the law speaks first — the claim met on its own ground; but the law's ground is paper, and paper favors whoever holds the most of it.)
- **Carry it to Augusta** — *the Dowager's reading, the family's.* She takes it to Augusta tonight — the Hartwell continuity weighed by its oldest keeper. (Consequence: the family's counsel — the oldest keeper reads it; but the Dowager reads the past, and the claim belongs to the present.)
> *(The choice goes into the ledger, and with it the manner of the choosing: the solitary reader, the solicitor's client, or the granddaughter. The claim was always going to be received. The signature — the how, the when, the with-whom — is hers.)*
