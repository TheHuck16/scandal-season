### L8.S1 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the masquerade's eve — the preparations; Paris, arrived. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The day before the masquerade, the house learned a new arithmetic. Rose walked the hall at four o'clock and counted: forty-two masks hung along the gallery wall, each tagged with a guest's name in Harrow's clerkly hand. Six hundred candles ordered, four hundred already in the sconces, the rest in the butler's pantry under cloths. Three musicians' stands by the east windows, tuned and waiting. She counted because counting steadied her, and because somebody had to know whether the night was ready.)*
> *(Paris had arrived before luncheon. The émigré houses came up the drive in a procession of hired carriages, the ladies with their jewel cases on their laps the way women carry things they do not trust to servants, the gentlemen bowing to the Park as if it were a person they were pleased to meet again. Harrow had put them in the guest wings, French beside French, and the corridors filled with a kind of French that Rose's schoolroom French could follow if she did not think about it too hard.)*
> *(In the hall the footmen were hanging the last of the candle branches. The wax smelled of honey and heat. A boy on a ladder reached too far and Harrow said, "Steady," without looking up from his list, and the boy steadied.)*
> *(Laurent found her by the great doors, watching the light come through the fan window in a shape like a hand.)*
> "It is nearly a ballroom," he said.
> "It was a ballroom last Tuesday," Rose said. "It has an excellent memory."
> *(He laughed, and the laugh was the unpolished one, the one that had taken him ten seasons to let her hear.)* "Tomorrow night," he said, quieter. "The old world's last night. And then, Rose —" He stopped, and looked at the masks on the wall, all those painted faces waiting. "Then the truth. I have worn the other thing so long I have forgotten where it ends and I begin."
> "Then it is a good thing it comes off," Rose said.
> "Yes." He took her hand and kissed it, not the Frenchman's flourish but something plainer. "Midnight. You will see."
> *(He went to write his letters to Paris, and Rose stood a while in the hall that smelled of wax and French soap, and counted the masks again. Forty-two. The old world, all present, one more night.)*

### L8.S2 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the masks — the choosing; the old world's ritual. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The masks were laid out on the long table in the morning room, on white cloths, like surgical instruments or jewels. Rose had asked for them brought down at noon, and Mrs. Quick had supervised the laying-out with the air of a woman arranging evidence.)*
> *(There were the three she had asked to see. The first quoted the S1 triumph: ivory silk, the same gold embroidery Élise's girls had stitched along the brow, the ribbon ties replaced because the old ones had gone soft with age. The second was the Hartwell rose, blush silk massed with small fabric roses at the temple, the work of it so fine Rose could see the stitches only if she held it to the light. The third was a plain black domino, unadorned, the silk so dark it seemed to take the light out of the room.)*
> *(She lifted each in turn. The triumph mask was light as a breath and smelled faintly of the cedar chest it had slept in. The rose mask was heavier, the little roses catching at her glove. The domino was nothing at all, weightless, honest.)*
> *(This was the old world's ritual, she supposed: choosing the face you would wear to the farewell. The Frenchwomen would have opinions. Letitia would have opinions. Octavia would look at the three and see something about Rose that Rose had not yet seen herself, and say it precisely, and be right.)*
> *(She held the domino up to her face and looked at herself in the glass over the mantel. A black oval, two eyes. It told nothing. It promised nothing. She set it down and picked up the triumph mask, and the gold embroidery caught the noon light, and for a moment she was twenty again, in the S1 gown, terrified and glittering, with the whole Season ahead of her like a road she could not see the end of.)*
> *(She put them all back on their cloths in a row. The choosing could wait for the night. That was the ritual's mercy: the masks would keep.)*
> *(Mrs. Quick folded the cloths' corners back with a precision that suggested she disapproved of indecision as a moral category. "Miss," she said, which was Mrs. Quick for everything else she was not saying, and withdrew.)*

### L8.S3 · [T] · Rose, Élise
*Purpose: the gown — the S1 triumph gown, quoted, transformed; the atelier's memory, worn. (Turns: 8)*
> *(Élise's fitting room smelled of chalk and hot irons and the orange-flower water Élise kept in a blue bottle for her own nerves. The gown hung on the stand like a held breath: ivory silk, the S1 triumph's lines, the embroidery requoted in gold along the hem and bodice where the old gown had carried it, the neckline lowered a fraction because Rose was twenty-five and not twenty, and knew the difference.)*
> *(Élise stood back with pins in her mouth, which made her look nineteen instead of twenty-two, and studied the hang of the skirt the way a general studies ground.)*
> "The triumph," Élise said around the pins, and took them out. "Quoted, Miss Hartwell. Transformed." She smoothed the silk over Rose's shoulder, and her chalk-stained hands were gentle. "The S1 gown was the beginning. Everybody remembers the beginning." She stepped back, head tilted. "This is the becoming. Walk like it."
> "I am standing still," Rose said.
> "Then stand like it." Élise smiled, and the hollow look she had worn all season was gone from her eyes. "The atelier's memory is in every stitch. The girls stayed late three nights. One of the girls cried over the hem." She adjusted a fold at the waist. "Happy tears. I told her to cry over the hem, it improves the drape."
> *(Rose laughed, and the gown moved with her, and it was true: the beginning, become. The ivory caught the lamplight and threw it back softened, like the gown was remembering something kind.)*
> *(On the worktable lay the bouquet, quoted too: the S1 triumph's flowers, transformed. White lilies for the purity of the intent, and among them small blush roses, the Hartwell's own. Élise had tied it with ivory ribbon and left the ends long.)*
> "For midnight," Élise said, following her look. "When the mask comes off. You will want something to hold."
> *(Rose picked up the bouquet. The lilies were cool against her gloves. She thought of the S1 ball, the terror, the glitter, and set the thought aside the way Élise set pins: carefully, and for later.)*

### L8.S4 · [D] · Rose, Laurent
*Purpose: Laurent, the eve — the Frenchman; the promise, tonight. (Turns: 8)*
> *(He found her in the gallery, between the ancestors, an hour before the guests were due down. The Hartwells on the walls looked at him with the particular skepticism of oil paint, and he looked back at them like a man facing a tribunal he had chosen.)*
> *(The gallery at that hour held the day's last light in its tall windows, and the dust moved in it, slow, unbothered. Somewhere below, a door closed, and a footman's footsteps crossed the hall and faded. The house was holding its breath before the evening.)*
> "Tonight," Laurent said, without preamble.
> "Tonight," Rose agreed. "The old world's last night. You have said so four times today. The ancestors are beginning to take it personally."
> *(He smiled, but it was the thin one, the polish worn through at the edges.)* "I practiced my English on the drive up. Your steward corrected my pronunciation of 'extinguisher.'"
> "Harrow corrects everyone's pronunciation. It is his love language."
> "Then I am much loved." *(He took her hands. His were cold.)* "Rose. I have been the Frenchman in England for —" He counted, frowning. "It does not matter how long. It feels like a life. The accent, the shrug, the —" He made a small gesture that was somehow entirely Parisian and entirely tired. "Tonight it ends. Not the man. The performance."
> "You will still shrug," Rose said. "I have seen you shrug in your sleep."
> "In my sleep I am French. It is allowed." *(He lifted her hands and held them against his chest, over his heart, which was beating fast enough that she could feel it through his coat.)* "At midnight the mask comes off. Permanently. I will stand in your hall, unmasked, and choose England, and your émigré guests will write home about it, and Paris will be furious or relieved, and none of it will matter." He looked at her directly, the unpolished look, the true one. "What matters is that you believe me."
> "I believe you," Rose said.
> "Good." He breathed out. "Then I can survive the ancestors."
> *(He bowed to the nearest Hartwell, a small ironic bow, and went down to dress, and Rose stood among the painted Hartwells and listened to his footsteps go, and to her own heart, which was beating nearly as fast as his. The dust went on moving in the light, unbothered, and she thought that was the trick of it: to be unbothered, and to go on.)*

### L8.S5 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the guests — Paris, arrived; the émigré houses, the old world. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The guests came at eight, and Rose watched them come from the long gallery's window, unobserved, still in her day dress, her hair not yet done. It was the hostess's privilege, this one hour of invisibility: to see the arrivals as a stranger would see them, before she became the evening's center.)*
> *(The carriages came up the drive in procession, lamps lit against the early dark. The émigré houses descended in their Parisian silks, the women with their jewel cases handed down like infants, the men straightening their orders on the steps. Harrow received them at the great doors with his ledger-calm, checking names against his list, directing footmen with small economical gestures. He had the evening's arithmetic entirely in hand, and Rose, watching, felt the particular peace of a household in competent hands.)*
> *(An old marquise paused on the steps and looked up at the house, and Rose saw her say something to her companion, and the companion nodded, and they went in together. A young French officer laughed at something, the sound carrying clear across the gravel. The English guests came among the French: neighbors, the ton, the curious, their carriages plainer, their jewels English. She watched a neighbor's wife descend carefully, holding her skirts clear of the wheel, and thought that arrivals were the most honest part of any evening: everyone still hoping, no one yet disappointed.)*
> *(She counted without meaning to: the carriages, the couples, the masks already tied on in the carriages, paint and silk flashing in the lamp light. Forty-one, forty-two. The old world, assembling for its farewell, and she the only soul in the house not yet dressed for it.)*
> *(Below, the great doors opened and closed, opened and closed. The hall filled with French. The night's first music — not the musicians yet, but the sound of arrival, talk and laughter rising like a tide — came up through the floorboards to where she stood.)*
> *(Her maid knocked, and came in, and laid out the ivory gown on the bed, and Rose turned from the window. The hour of invisibility was ending. The evening wanted its hostess, and the hostess had a mask to choose.)*

### L8.S6 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the hall — the masquerade's stage; the old world's theater. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The hall at half past eight, before the company came down: the masquerade's stage, set. Rose walked its length alone, in the ivory gown now, her hair done, the mask not yet chosen — that waited in the morning room, on its white cloths — and the hall received her the way a theater receives its actress before the audience is admitted.)*
> *(Six hundred candles, lit. The stands by the east windows, the musicians' chairs empty, the instruments waiting. The masks on the gallery wall, all claimed now, the tags removed. The supper room beyond, under cloths. The chandelier's crystals threw small rainbows onto the empty floor, and her footsteps sounded loud and then not loud, the way footsteps do in a room built for music.)*
> *(She stood at its center and turned slowly, taking the stage's measure. This was the old world's theater, and tonight it would hold the old world's farewell. The French airs would come from the alcove. The dancers would turn where she stood. At midnight the masks would come off, and the faces underneath would blink in the candlelight, and Laurent's face among them, unmasked permanently.)*
> *(She thought of the S1 ball, ten seasons past: this hall, this chandelier, herself terrified at the stair's foot. The girl had not known the hall was a stage. The woman knew, and knew too that the stage was hers to set. The candles, the music, the supper, the guest list — the arithmetic of the night, all balanced, all hers.)*
> *(A footman crossed with a final branch of candles, and Harrow followed, checking, and Harrow saw her and bowed, and did not ask why the hostess was alone in her hall. Harrow understood the hour before the evening. Everyone who has ever run a house understands it: the stillness before the doors open. Above, the ancestors looked down from the gallery, oil-paint eyes on the empty floor, and Rose nodded to them, hostess to hosts, and they approved, or she chose to think so.)*
> *(She went up the stair then, to the morning room, where the three masks waited on their white cloths. The stage was set. The choosing remained.)*

### L8.S7 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the music — the old world's; the masquerade's sound. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The music was warming up. Rose paused by the east alcove's doorway, unobserved, and listened to the musicians tune: the first violin's A, the cello's answering C, a flute trying a run and catching it. The thin man with the wart on his chin — the first violin — tapped his bow against his stand and they began a French air, quietly, for themselves, not yet for the hall.)*
> *(It was a gavotte, the one her dancing master had despaired of, and hearing it played soft, as a rehearsal, undid something in her. The old world's music, practicing its farewell. The émigré ladies would close their eyes when they heard it, she knew. A tune could do what words could not: carry a country across the water.)*
> *(The cellist smiled at something the flutist did, and they tried the passage again, and got it, and the first violin nodded, satisfied. They were good, these musicians. Harrow had chosen them, or the choice had been made by someone who understood that a farewell needs a tune you can hum.)*
> *(She thought of all the music of ten seasons: the S1 ball's nervous strings, the Vauxhall gala's brass, the Bath assembly's thin piano. This was different. This was the sound of the old world tuning itself for the last time, and she stood in the doorway and let it wash over her, the hostess not yet the hostess, the night not yet the night.)*
> *(The gavotte ended. The first violin tapped his bow. They began a musette air, sweet and a little sad, and Rose thought of Laurent upstairs, dressing, his letters to Paris written, his English practiced, and of how a tune could hold what he was leaving, and she let it hold her, there in the doorway, until Harrow's footsteps in the hall told her the company was coming down. The flutist caught her eye as she turned to go, and smiled, quick and shy, and she smiled back, and the musette air followed her up the stair like a hand waving.)*
> *(She slipped away before they saw her. The musicians played on, unaware, and the hall filled behind her with talk and silk, and the masquerade's sound became the night's.)*

### L8.S8 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the unmasking's eve — the anticipation; midnight, approaching. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The ball began below without her, which was the hostess's prerogative and also her favorite part. Rose stood in the morning room with the three masks on their white cloths and listened to it assemble through the floorboards: the first proper French air, the dancers finding their places, the murmur rising like water coming to a boil.)*
> *(Midnight was two hours off. Two hours of silk and paint, and then the ribbons untied, and then whatever was underneath, blinking in the candlelight. Laurent's underneath first among them. She had believed him in the gallery with his heart hammering under her palm, and she believed him still, but belief, she was discovering, was not a door you walked through once. It was a lamp you trimmed every evening.)*
> *(Forty-two masks hung along the gallery wall for forty-two guests, Harrow's clerkly tags on every one. Hers were the only three laid out for choosing, which struck her as either an honor or a symptom. The old world's ritual: pick the face you wear to the farewell. The Frenchwomen would have opinions. Letitia would have several. Octavia would look at the three and say something precise about Rose that Rose had not yet said to herself, and be right, and Rose was in no hurry to hear it.)*

### L8.S9 · ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — THE MASK (which mask Rose wears: the triumph quoted, the rose, or the domino plain). The night deepens; decide the mask. (Turns: 3 — the decision)*
> *(The night deepened, and the masks waited on their white cloths in the morning room, and Rose stood before them the way a general regards three roads. The triumph, quoted: ivory silk, gold embroidery, the beginning's face. The rose: blush silk, the little fabric roses, the Hartwell legacy blooming at the temple. The domino, plain: black silk, unadorned, the woman's face with nothing on it.)*
> *(She had handled them at noon and set them aside, and the setting aside had been its own kind of choosing, a postponement dressed as deliberation. Now the ball was below her, the French airs coming up through the floor, and the night wanted its first statement.)*
> *(The triumph mask would say: I remember who I was. The beginning, quoted, the S1 girl become the woman, the past worn as proof. It would match the gown, ivory and gold, the quoted triumph head to foot. The old world would understand it at once. Everyone who remembered would understand.)*
> *(The rose mask would say: I am the Hartwell. The legacy, the house, the name, blooming. It would be the bolder choice, the blush against the ivory gown, the flower the house had bred across ten seasons. Letitia would approve. Augusta would approve. It would be a statement, and the night wanted a statement.)*
> *(The domino would say nothing, which was its own statement. Plain black silk. No quotation, no legacy. The woman, unadorned, at the old world's farewell. It would be the quietest face in the hall, and the truest, and nobody would understand it but her.)*
> *(She thought of Élise's hands, chalk-stained, tying the bouquet. She thought of Laurent's cold hands, his heart beating through his coat. She thought of midnight, two hours off, when all the masks would come off anyway, and the choosing would be revealed as what it was: a few hours' loan of a face.)*
> *(The French airs rose through the floor, sweet and insistent. The masks waited. The night held its breath, politely, the way the old world did everything.)*

### L8.S11 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the dancing — masked; the old world's last dance. (Turns: 7)*
> *(She danced. The mask she had chosen moved with her face, the ribbons holding, the silk breathing. Her partners were a succession of silk and paint: a French vicomte who danced as if the floor owed him money, an English squire's son who apologized to her slippers, Laurent twice, masked, his hand sure at her waist.)*
> *(The old world's last dance went on around her, the hall turning, the French airs. The women's skirts swung, the men's coats flared, the candlelight moved over it all. Somebody's mask slipped and was straightened with laughter. Somebody guessed somebody else wrongly and there was a shriek of delight.)*
> *(Rose thought, dancing, of the S1 ball: the terror, the newness, the feeling of being looked at by a hundred eyes and found wanting or not. > *(Between dances she stood by the pillar and drank champagne and watched. The émigré ladies danced with a particular grace, as if each dance were a small act of remembrance. ### L8.S12 · [D] · Rose, Laurent
*Purpose: Laurent, masked — the Frenchman, the night's; the truth, underneath. (Turns: 8)*
> *(He claimed her for the dance before midnight, and the hall made way for them the way halls do for couples it has decided to watch. Masked, the Frenchman, the night's, his hand at her waist sure and warm through the ivory silk.)*
> "The hall," he said as they turned, surveying it over her shoulder. "Full. The émigré —" A glance at the French contingent, laughing by the windows. "Paris, here. The old world —" Another glance, at the English. "One more night."
> "You have been counting," Rose said.
> "I count when I am nervous. It is a habit I learned from an Englishwoman." *(The mask's eyes smiled, or the eyes behind the mask did.)* "Midnight, Rose. The unmasking."
> "I know. You have mentioned it."
> "I will mention it again. It is the only subject I have." *(He spun her, neatly, and caught her, neatly, and the hall approved with a small sound.)* "The mask off. Permanently. England."

### L8.S13 · [T] · Rose (alone)
