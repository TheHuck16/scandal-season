### L11.S4 · plot beat · Rose (alone) — "The Posture's Question"
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — LEGAL POSTURE (fight, settle, or out-wait). Decide before the week decides for her. (Turns: 3 — the decision)*
> *(The morning room, after Quill. The claim lies on the desk, unread — she has decided to read it after she decides, not before. The seal is unbroken; the breaking of it can wait for the choosing. Three roads, and the law's menu in order: posture first. Augusta would read first and decide after. Augusta is not the one being sued. She prices them the way she prices everything: once for the sum, once for the nerve.)*
*★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — Legal Posture:*
- **Fight** — *Fight it in the open.* The defense argued where the town can watch — Sloane at the table, Quill at his elbow, the evidence squared and the witnesses rehearsed, the whole proceeding conducted where the light is best. (Consequence: a public defense is an honest one, which is its strength and its cost — the Gazette will print every blow that lands, and some will land.)
- **Settle** — *Buy the claim off.* A sum named, a paper signed, the claimant's gloves disappearing down the corridor for the last time, the whole matter filed under finished. (Consequence: the house gets its quiet back, and quiet is a currency — but a settlement looks like an admission, and the town will read it however the town pleases.)
- **Out-wait** — *Let the paper ripen or rot.* No hurry, no headlines. The house goes about its week, the law goes about its slowness, and the claimant learns what waiting costs when the waiting is mutual. (Consequence: dignity costs nothing and buys time — but the claimant's paper keeps working while she waits, and patience is only a virtue when it wins.)
> *(The game remembers the choice — and the means: the fighter, the settler, or the unrushed. The posture was always going to be chosen. The signature is hers, and the ink is dry.)*
