*Purpose: Texture — Rose alone with the obligations list; the shape of what is being bought.*

### L19.S6 · plot beat · Rose Hartwell
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — THE PURCHASE. Before she can move, the purchase needs a face: bid openly as Hartwell, through Trelawney's name, or through a London agent.*
> That evening Rose walked the Crescent alone, doing the bankers' arithmetic a day early — the arithmetic of faces.
> *(System: the purchase needs a face before it needs a price. Three faces are available; each costs differently.)*
> Bid openly, as Hartwell — her name on the purchase before the banking houses of Bath. The nerve of it was the statement Augusta demanded. The cost of it would be the exposure: the Network would know, that night, exactly who had bloodied it, and the principal — the de Valcourt interest, the hand behind the glove — would learn her name the way one learns an enemy's.
> Bid through Trelawney — the banker's name on the paper, hers behind it. Respectable, deniable, and exactly what the front would expect a frightened heiress to do. The cost: Trelawney's nerve, which was real but finite, and the whiff of fear about the whole proceeding — the ton can smell a cutout the way it smells a lie.
> Bid through a London agent — Quill's man, or one of Augusta's, a professional buyer with no Bath connections and no face the front would recognize. The safest. The most deniable. And the most *shadowed* — the very thing Augusta's condition forbade: business done in a solicitor's office, not a statement made in daylight.
> The Crescent lamps came on one by one. Below, Bath glittered — the town that had underestimated her all season, and was about to be revised.
> *(System: the choice is Rose's. The block cannot be laid until the purchase has a face.)*
>
> **[T1 · Look]** — The open face: *weigh its nerve / weigh its exposure / weigh Augusta's demand.* (Remembered: what tempts her — nerve or safety.)
> **[T2 · Look]** — Trelawney's face: *weigh his nerve / weigh the whiff of fear / weigh the bankers' read.* (Remembered: how she prices other people's courage.)
> **[T3 · Look]** — The agent's face: *weigh its safety / weigh its shadow / weigh Augusta's condition.* (Remembered: whether the condition binds her.)
> **[T4 · Stance]** — "The principal would learn her name": *welcome it / dread it / price it.* (Remembered: her appetite for being known.)

*★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — The purchase's face:*
- **Bid openly as Hartwell** — *Nerve+, exposure.* Rose's name on the purchase, in daylight, before the houses; the statement Augusta demanded — and the principal learns her name that night.
- **Bid through Trelawney's name** — *Deniability, bankers' nerve spent.* The banker's name fronts it; respectable, expected — with the faint whiff of fear the ton will detect.
- **Bid through a London agent** — *Safety, shadow.* A professional buyer, no Bath face; the safest course — and the least daylight, against Augusta's condition.

### L19.S9 · [T] · Rose Hartwell
*Purpose: Texture — the counting-house after hours: ledgers, ink, the smell of money gone quiet; the paper's human cost.*
> Trelawney kept her past the clerk's leaving, and Rose sat a while among the ledgers after the plan was drawn, listening to the counting-house settle.
> *(System: the room at dusk — the day's entries ruled off, the strongbox locked, the inkstands capped; money, gone quiet for the night, like a dog that has been fed.)*
> The ledgers stood in rows, each spine a year, each year a hundred small disasters survived or not. She ran a finger along 1794 — the year of the bad harvest note, her father's flourish — and thought of all the hands that had written in these books: clerks dead forty years, the émigré names in faded continental hands, endorsing paper that had crossed the Channel in fishing boats and ended here, in ink.
> Money had a smell, she decided: ink and dust and the faint, sweet rot of old paper. It smelled nothing like the ballroom's orange water. And yet the ballroom and the counting-house were the same war now — the assembly rooms lit for dancing, the ledgers ruled for lending, and between them a girl in a muslin walking both floors with a war chest in her pocketbook.
> "You are thinking," said Trelawney from the doorway, "that it is a great deal of trouble for ink."
> "I am thinking," said Rose, "that ink is the only thing in this town that tells the truth. Paper does not know it is lying. People do, and charge extra for it."
> He laughed — the first unbanked sound she had heard from him. "Quill's line. He uses it on everyone."
> "It is a good line. I intend to steal it." She rose. "Thursday, then. The dinner. And the week after — the assembly."
> "The assembly," he agreed, and locked the strongbox with a sound like a period at the end of a sentence.
