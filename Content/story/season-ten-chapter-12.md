### L12.S1 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the charter, continued — the founding's work; the morning, the written. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The library at seven, and the charter continued — the founding's work, taken up where the night left it. Rose sits at the desk with the household book open to the blank pages at the back, and the heading she wrote a week ago —* The Hartwell Assembly *— stands at the top of the page like a door not yet opened.)*
> *(She reads it over once, the way she reads everything: for the true weight. The heading is a promise. This morning she begins to keep it. The pen — the good pen, the one Quill gave her when the settlement was enrolled — moves across the page in her ledger-hand, and the charter takes its first shape: the annual house party and ball, convened by her, on her terms.)*
> *(The terms come first, because terms are the spine. Not a Season's entertainment, hired and forgotten. An *institution*: the house party in summer, the ball at its height, the guests convened — not invited, *convened* — by the mistress of Hartwell Park. She writes it plainly, the way Quill writes: no ornament, no apology. The plainness is the point. Ornament can be argued with. Plainness can only be answered.)*
> *(A footman brings chocolate, and she drinks it without tasting it, the way she has drunk chocolate through ten seasons of mornings that mattered. Outside, the Park does its morning work — the gardeners at the beds, the grooms at the yard — unaware that inside the library, at the desk, the ton's future is being chartered one line at a time.)*
> "The annual," she says aloud, testing it. The word holds. "The *convened*." That holds too. She writes them both into the charter's margin, and underlines them once — not twice, the way Lavinia underlines; once is enough for a founding.
> *(By eight the first page is full, and the second begun, and the founding's work — the morning, the written — has the particular momentum of things that were always going to happen. The Assembly is taking shape. The charter is being written. And Rose — twenty-five, mistress of the finished whole — is doing what she has always done: building the institution first, and letting the brilliance follow.)*

### L12.S2 · [D] · Rose, Letitia
*Purpose: the quartermaster, on the founding — Letitia, the charter's; strategy, the annual's. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The morning room at nine, and Letitia summoned to inspect the charter. She arrives with the Gazette folded unopened under her arm, and Rose understands this is the highest compliment in the aunt's repertoire: the news can wait. The founding cannot.)*
> "Darling." Letitia reads the pages through twice, the hawk's eyes moving fast. She sets them down with unusual care. "Well. The quartermaster approves."
> "Entirely?" Rose pours the chocolate. "Or with the usual reservations, to be revealed at the worst possible moment?"
> "Entirely." Letitia cradles her cup, and the warm look comes with the grave one underneath. "Do you know why? Because it is the masterstroke, darling. Ten seasons we have *attended* things — balls, routs, breakfasts, other people's brilliance on other people's terms." She taps the charter with one finger. "Now they come to *us*. Once a year. On *your* terms."
> "The terms are still being written."
> "Then write them boldly." She pulls the household book toward her — the quartermaster cannot plan a campaign without the book — and opens a fresh page. "The house party in summer, when the Park is at its greenest. The ball at the full moon, when the drive lights itself." She writes swiftly. "The guests convened, not invited. There is a difference, darling, and the difference is power."
> "And the cost?"
> "Harrow will do the figures." Letitia does not look up. "Harrow *loves* figures. It is his entire personality."
> "The dragon," Letitia says, tapping the charter's list, "is first, darling. Before the ton, before the many." The hawk's eyes go grave. "The guest-list war ended in the spring, but wars have aftermaths. Convene her first, by your own hand, and the aftermath becomes the peace."
> "By hand," Rose says. "This afternoon."
> "This afternoon." Letitia writes it in the book. "The dragon, convened first. The ton, convened after." She looks up. "It is strategy, darling, but it is also courtesy. The two are not enemies, whatever the old may tell you."
> *(They laugh together, and the charter gains its first ally. Letitia reaches for the Gazette at last, unfolds it one-handed, and reads the first leader with the air of a woman rewarding herself after a campaign well begun.)*
> "Darling." The hawk's eyes do not lift from the page. "Bell has opinions about the new opera house. Bell is *wrong* about the new opera house." She turns a page. "The founding, darling — the founding is *right*."
> *(Outside the window the Park goes about its morning, unaware that the year's campaign has just been planned over chocolate and the quartermaster's marginalia.)*

### L12.S3 · [D] · Rose, Augusta
*Purpose: the Dowager, on the convening — Augusta, the charter's; the line, the annual's. (Turns: 8)*
> *(Augusta's morning room at ten, and the Dowager receiving the charter the way she receives everything: seated, spectacled, seventy-two years fully deployed, with the air of a judge who has already written the verdict and is merely waiting for the evidence to catch up.)*
> "The Assembly." Augusta reads the pages slowly, the spectacles low on her nose. "Annual." She turns a page. "A house party and ball." Another page. "Convened —" a pause — "by you, child. On your terms." She sets the charter down. The rarest smile.
> "And the line's verdict, Grandmama?"
> "The line —" the voice does something extraordinary, going soft at the edges — "the line *convenes*, child." Augusta removes the spectacles — twice a year, perhaps — and looks at Rose with unguarded eyes. "Do you know what your grandfather would have said to this? *Break the entail*, he said, *to save the house*. He broke it. Your father let it rot, to punish it." She replaces the spectacles. "And you, child —" the dry verdict — "you have *finished* it. And now you convene the ton to *witness* it. Annually."
> "The dragon," Rose says. "She must be first."
> "The dragon —" Augusta's mouth does something nearly warm — "the dragon will come, child. To be convened." She takes Rose's hand — the old hand, the rings, the weight. "The ton, child, will come. They will come because it is *annual*, and because it is *yours*, and because —" the driest pause — "because no one in London will dare to be the *one* who was not convened."
> "The seal." Augusta holds out her hand, and Rose gives her the signet — the Hartwell oak, the grandmother's. The Dowager turns it in the light. "Your grandfather broke the entail with *this* hand." She closes her fingers around the seal. "Not the seal. The *hand*." She returns it. "Use it, child. Sparingly." The driest pause. "Except for *this*. For this —" the rarest smile — "use it *annually*."
> "Grandmama." Rose hesitates. "The terms. Are they —" she searches — "are they *bold* enough?"
> "The terms —" Augusta considers the pages — "the terms are *plain*, child. Boldness is for *battles*. Plainness is for *institutions*." She rises, the audience concluded. "The line convenes. The charter *holds*. Write it fair, child, and seal it *red*."
> *(Rose holds the old hand, and the Dowager's receiving stands — the line's, the spectacles', the annual's — and the charter, which had a quartermaster at nine, has a dynasty by ten.)*

### L12.S4 · [D] · Rose, Harrow
*Purpose: the steward, the charter's practicalities — Harrow, the annual's costs. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The estate office at eleven, and Harrow with his books open — the steward's reckoning. He lays the convening's costs out the way he lays out everything: in columns, in order, without ornament. The house party: the beds, the boards, the servants' extra wages. The ball: the candles, the musicians, the supper. The annual: the whole of it, multiplied by forever.)*
> "The annual, Miss Hartwell." He turns a page with the care of a man handling scripture. "The house party. The ball. The convening." The dry smile. "I have done the figures three ways —" he taps the columns — "the modest, the proper, and the *Lavinia*."
> "And the verdict?"
> "The Park can bear it, miss." He looks up, the historian's eyes meeting hers. "Annually." He closes the book with a soft, definitive sound. "The charter is sound, miss."
> "Even the *Lavinia* way?"
> "Even the Lavinia way, miss." A beat. "Though I have taken the liberty of costing the *proper* way, and leaving the Lavinia way as a *supplement*." The dry smile deepens. "For occasions of particular brilliance."
> *(Rose looks at the columns and does her own arithmetic: once for the sum, once for the nerve. The candles alone run to a figure that would have stopped her heart in the first season. She does not flinch. She has learned what things cost and what they are worth, and the two ledgers are not the same.)*
> "The charter is sound," she says. "Write it so, Mr. Harrow. In the book. *Sound*."
> "The musicians." Harrow turns to a marked page. "The full number, miss. The proper way." The dryest. "The Lavinia way is the full number —" he consults the column — "with *trumpets*."
> "Trumpets," Rose says faintly.
> "Trumpets, miss." He closes the book on the supplement. "Costed, miss. As a supplement. For occasions of particular brilliance." He looks at her over the pages. "The founding, miss, may qualify."
> "The founding will begin with the *proper* way. No trumpets." She pauses. "The *first* year."
> "The first year, miss." Harrow writes it down — the historian's hand, the pensioner's satisfaction. "The trumpets wait, miss. Annually."
> *(She leaves him to his columns, and the office door shuts on the soft sound of his pencil resuming — the man already costing next year's candles, the founding's first honest debt, entered before the ink of the charter is dry.)*

### L12.S5 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the terms — the Assembly's terms; convened, not joined. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The library at noon, and the terms — written fair. Rose copies the charter's spine onto a clean page, the three terms that will govern every year of the convening, and reads each one aloud as she writes it, testing the weight the way Quill tests a clause.)*
> *(The first term: the Assembly is annual — the house party in summer, the ball at the full moon — fixed, returning, forever. Not a Season's whim. An institution. She writes it, and the word* annual *sits on the page like a cornerstone.)*
> *(The second term: the Assembly is convened — by the mistress of Hartwell Park, on her terms. Not joined, not attended. Convened. The ton has spent a century inviting itself to itself. The Assembly turns the invitation around: they come to *her*, or they do not come at all.)*
> *(The third term: the guests are the convened — the family, the lovers, the ton, the dragon — named by the convener, welcomed by the house. No one convenes himself. No one arrives unlooked-for. The house party belongs to the Park; the ball belongs to the night; the terms belong to her.)*
> *(She reads the three together, and they hold. Letitia was right about the masterstroke. The terms do not merely organize a party. They declare a power: the power to convene. For ten seasons she has earned the house. The terms earn the future.)*
> *(Letitia comes in at two with the luncheon tray and reads the three terms over Rose's shoulder.)*
> "Darling." The hawk's eyes move down the page. "The *terms*." She reads the first again. "Annual." The second. "Convened." The third. "The convened." She straightens. "It is —" the warm look — "it is *Quill*, darling. Plain as a *brief*."
> "Quill," Rose says, "would charge for the plainness."
> "Quill —" Letitia laughs — "Quill would charge for the *ink*, darling." She taps the page. "But the terms are *yours*. Not joining the ton. Convening it." She writes in the margin, in the quartermaster's hand: *Three terms. Annual, convened, the convened. The charter's spine.* "Most foundings are jelly, darling. This one has bones."
> *(She sands the page dry and sits back, and the library holds the noon the way it holds everything: patiently. The terms are written, and they will bear the weight of every year to come — the house party, the ball, and the woman who convenes them.)*

### L12.S6 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the date — the annual's; the choosing, the fixed. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The library at one, and the date — to be chosen and fixed. Rose turns the household book to the year's calendar, and the months lie before her like guests waiting to be convened.)*
> *(June: the roses, the longest days, the Park at its greenest — but June belongs to the Season's height, and the Assembly is not the Season's. It stands beside the Season, above it. July: the heat, the ton fled to the country — the wrong country, other people's houses. August: the grouse, the men distracted, the young restless.)*
> *(She considers the moon, the way Letitia taught her to consider everything: as a guest. The full moon lights the drive for nothing. It has attended every great night of the ten seasons, uninvited and indispensable. The ball wants the full moon. The charter wants the fixed.)*
> *(Letitia comes in with the luncheon tray and reads the deliberation off Rose's face.)*
> "Darling." She sets the tray down. "You are choosing a *date* as if it were a *husband*. It will repeat, darling. It need only be right once."
> "That is exactly why it must be right." Rose taps the calendar. "The full moon. Late summer. After the Season's thin end, before the autumn's shooting." She looks up. "The young will be restless. The Park will be golden. The drive will light itself."
> "Late summer." Letitia writes it in the book. "The full moon." The hawk's eyes gleam. "Darling, the moon is a guest too, and she will want the good candles — annually." She caps her pen. "Do you know what the ton will say? They will say the Hartwell Assembly is *fixed* — the way the almanac is fixed."
> "The almanac," Rose says, "does not convene."
> "The almanac —" Letitia rises, pausing at the door — "does not *dare*, darling." The warm look. "Late summer. The full moon. Yearly." She touches the doorframe. "The date is chosen, darling, and it will not move. The Assembly has its *when*."
> *(Rose writes it into the charter's margin —* late summer, the full moon, yearly *— and underlines it once. Through the window the Park lies in its afternoon order, and somewhere above the oaks the moon is already rising toward a date it does not yet know it has been given.)*

### L12.S7 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the guests — the first list; the convened, the named. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The library at two, and the guests — the first list, to be convened and named. Rose takes a clean page and writes the headings the way Quill writes a brief: in order, without sentiment, the sentiment to be supplied by the event itself.)*
> *(The family: Augusta — the Dowager, the line, seventy-two and indispensable. Letitia — the quartermaster, the aunt, the household book's keeper. The family's convening is not a courtesy. It is the foundation. The Assembly stands on the family the way the Park stands on its stone.)*
> *(The lovers: Julian — the free man, the mended gloves in his pocket. Laurent — the Englishman, the unmasked, the direct. The heart's convening is not a decoration. It is the proof: that the convener is loved, and loves, and convenes from the center of a life, not the edge of one.)*
> *(The ton: the great houses — the Almerias, the Craykes, the Micklethwaites — the young, in their dozens, orbiting. The many are the point: the Assembly convenes the ton because the ton is the audience, the witness, the reason the charter was written at all.)*
> *(The dragon: Lady Agatha Drummond. First. Before the ton, before the many — the dragon is convened *first*, by hand, by the convener herself. The guest-list war ended in the spring; the charter seals the peace in ink. She writes the name last and underlines it once.)*
> *(She reads the dragon's name once more — underlined once, the way foundings underline — and thinks of the spring: the private word, on her terms; the guest list, surrendered; the war, ended.)*
> *(Honoria's name is not on the list — the daughter comes with the dragon, the way the tide comes with the moon — but Rose writes it in the margin anyway, in the small hand:* Honoria — convened as herself. *The margin holds what the list cannot: that the convening is personal, even when it is institutional.)*
> *(The page is full. The list is exact. Every name on it is a load-bearing wall, and the dragon's — underlined once — is the cornerstone. Rose sands the page, and the Assembly has its company.)*

### L12.S8 · [D] · Rose, Lady Agatha Drummond
*Purpose: the dragon, the first guest — Drummond, the convened; the peace, the annual's. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The morning room at three, and Drummond — the dragon, the first guest, received alone. Rose has kept this hour empty the way she keeps the good candles for the full moon: deliberately. The invitation lies on the table between them, sealed, the wax still soft.)*
> *(Lady Agatha Drummond takes it with the exact hands — the hands that once held the guest list like a weapon, in the spring, before the private word on Rose's terms ended the war. She breaks the seal slowly. She reads. The room is very quiet.)*
> "Lady Drummond." Rose holds the exact courtesy, the charter's own. "The Hartwell Assembly —" she nods at the invitation — "annual. A house party and ball." She looks at the dragon — the equal, the reconciled. "Convened by me. On my terms." A breath. "The peace. Annually."
> *(Drummond reads the invitation twice — the first time for the terms, the second time for the woman — and when she looks up, the honest look is there, the one from the spring.)*
> "The Assembly." Drummond sets the invitation down with care. "Convened." The honest look, held. "Miss Hartwell —" the rarest — "Rose." A pause. "The peace, annually." She takes Rose's hand. "I accept." The smallest smile, the dragon's, the true one's. "The dragon is convened."
> *(They sit a moment in the quiet — the convener and the convened, the war ended, the peace chartered — and Rose understands what the first guest means: the Assembly is not a defiance. It is a settlement. The dragon, convened first, makes the convening unassailable.)*
> *(Drummond rises, the invitation in her hand, and at the door she turns — the dragon's own pause, the one that once preceded terms and conditions.)*
> "Miss Hartwell." The exact voice, the honest look. "The *private word*, in the spring, was on your terms, Rose. This —" she holds up the invitation — "this is on your terms, *annually*." The smallest smile. "The dragon is *convened*."
> "Annually, Rose." The dragon's bow — the exact, the true. "I shall *come*."
> *(She goes, and the morning room holds its quiet, and Rose sits a moment with the empty table — the seal, the wax, the gone. The founding's hardest convening, accomplished before the ink is dry.)*

### L12.S9 · ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — THE CHARTER'S FIRST ARTICLE (the Assembly's founding principle: the house, the convening, or the annual). The morning deepens; decide the principle. (Turns: 3 — the decision)*
> *(The library at dusk, and the charter asking its first article. Rose stands at the window with the draft pages in her hand, and the morning deepens around the question — no, the *afternoon* deepens; the founding has taken all day, and the light is going.)*
> *(The house: the Park's, the stone's. The first article could name the house — Hartwell Park, the stone, the whole, the finished. The principle would be the stone's. But stones are still, and the Assembly — she pictures them arriving, the young laughing, the dragon exact — the Assembly is the *gathering*. A house cannot gather. Only people gather.)*
> *(The convening: the ton's, the gathered. The first article could name the convening — the ton, gathered, on her terms. The principle would be the gathered's. But gatherings are people, and the Assembly — she pictures the charter's pages, the seal, the date fixed — the Assembly is the *annual*. People scatter. The charter returns.)*
> *(The annual: the year's, the returning. The first article could name the annual — the year, the returning, forever. The principle would be the returning's. But returnings are future, and the Assembly — she touches the draft pages, the ink still fresh — the Assembly is the *now*: this charter, this seal, this founding.)*
> *(Letitia comes in with the evening post and reads the indecision off Rose's face.)*
> "Darling." The hawk's eyes, amused. "The *first article*, darling. The charter's *spine*." She sets down the post. "The house is the *where*. The convening is the *what*. The annual is the *when*." She taps the draft. "A first article, darling, is the *why*."
> "The why," Rose says slowly, "is —" she looks at the pages — "that the ton is *convened*, by the *house*, *annually*." She picks up the pen. "The house, the convening, the annual." The pen hovers. "All three are true. But a first article —" she looks at Letitia — "a first article names *one*."
> "One, darling." The hawk's eyes, warm. "The founding's spine. Choose it boldly."
> *(The pen waits over the page — the house, the convening, the annual, all three true, only one the first article's. Outside, the Park darkens into its evening order, and inside the library the founding waits on its *why* — and the pen is in her hand, and the book lies open to receive it.)*

### L12.S10 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the writing — the charter, written; the founding, the inked. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The library at four, and the writing — the charter, written fair. Rose takes the drafts — the morning's pages, Letitia's annotations, Augusta's verdict, Harrow's figures — and copies the whole into the household book in her ledger-hand: the fair copy, the permanent one.)*
> *(The pen moves steadily: the heading, the terms, the date, the first list. The ink is the good ink, the paper the Park's, the hand — Quill once called it the only honest thing in Chancery — exact. She writes the way the house was built: one deliberate thing at a time, until suddenly it is all at once.)*
> *(Letitia comes in midway and watches over her shoulder, the quartermaster's inspection.)*
> "Darling." The hawk's eyes on the page. "The fair copy." She reads a line. "The *terms*." Another line. "The *date*." She straightens. "It is —" the warm look — "it is *beautiful*, darling. Not the hand —" a laugh — "though the hand is *adequate* — the *thing*. The charter."
> "The first article," Rose says, not looking up, "is as I chose. The house, the convening, the annual — whichever the morning decided." She writes on.
> "The morning," Letitia says, "decided *all three*, darling. That is what foundings do. They *include*." She watches the pen. "Write it fair. The household book has waited ten seasons for a page like this."
> *(The pen falters once — on the date, late summer, the full moon — and Rose stops, the nib hovering.)*
> "Darling." Letitia, watching. "The *date*. It is only the annual's."
> "It is the *when*." Rose writes it — late summer, the full moon, yearly — and the nib moves on. "It must be exact."
> "Exact." Letitia reads the line. "The moon is a guest too, darling, and she will want the good candles." The hawk's eyes, warm. She touches the margin. "Write it fair, darling. The household book has waited ten seasons for a page like this — and the *margin* has waited for the quartermaster's hand." She writes, in the small decisive script: *Fair copy, the founding's. Witnessed by the quartermaster.*
> *(The writing goes on into the evening — the pen steady, the pages filling — and the household book fills the way the Park filled: completely, and as a promise kept. When the last line is sanded dry, Rose sits back, and the book lies open, and the Assembly exists as ink on paper.)*

### L12.S11 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the sealing — the charter, sealed; the founding, the permanent. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The library at dusk, and the sealing. Rose heats the wax with the steady hand of ten seasons' practice, and the stick softens, and the first drop falls — red, the Park's own — onto the charter's final page.)*
> *(The seal is the Hartwell oak — the signet, the grandmother's, the one Augusta pressed into her hand in the spring with the words* use it sparingly, child *— and she presses it now, firmly, into the wax. The oak rises in red. She holds the seal a moment longer than necessary, feeling the metal warm through the handle.)*
> *(She thinks of all the seals of the ten seasons — the Network's paper, the entail's true history, Quill's final enrollment — and of this one, different from all of them: a *beginning*. The first seal she has ever pressed for joy.)*
> *(Letitia, summoned to witness, stands at the desk with the air of a godmother at a christening.)*
> "Darling." The hawk's eyes on the wax. "The seal." She touches the cooled edge with one finger. "The oak." The warm look. "Do you know what you have done? You have *pressed* the future, darling. Like a flower." A laugh. "A *very* expensive flower, annually."
> "The founding," Rose says, "is sealed."
> *(The wax cools, and the oak stands in red. Rose looks at the seal a long moment, the signet warm in her hand.)*
> "The *first* seal." Letitia, very quietly. "For joy."
> "The first." Rose sets the signet down. "The others were —" she searches — "defenses. Settlements." She touches the cooled wax. "This one is a *beginning*."
> "Beginnings —" Letitia closes the household book on the sealed page, with the particular tenderness she reserves for finished things — "beginnings are the quartermaster's favorite maneuver, darling. They require everything." The warm look. "And they *return* everything." She ties the book's ribbon. "The wax —" she says it the way other women say *amen* — "is pressed."
> *(The ribbon tied, the book closed, the room holds its dusk. Rose carries the sealed charter to the strongbox herself — no footman, no ceremony — and turns the key twice, the way the house has always turned its keys: once for the lock, once for the keeping.)*

### L12.S12 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the announcing — London, informed; the convening, the declared. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The library the next morning, and the announcing. Rose sits with the fair copies — the charter's public face, the letters of announcement — and the pen moves again, but differently now: the convener's hand, the one that declares.)*
> *(The letters go first to the family — Augusta, Letitia, already convened in person — then to the lovers, then to the ton's great houses, then to the young.
