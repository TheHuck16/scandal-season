### L11.S1 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the letter, answered — Rose replies; the planning, begun. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The library before breakfast, and the night post lying on the desk like a small white accuser. Rose sorts it the way Letitia taught her — the bills at the bottom, the interesting at the top — and the interesting is Lavinia's letter, which announces itself before she opens it: cream paper, handwriting slanted at a gallop, the seal pressed crooked because Lavinia never could wait for wax to cool.)*
> *(She reads it twice. The first time for the sense, the second time for Lavinia, who is in every line the way perfume is in a room. A final fête, the letter says. Her last. The Season's young are restless, she writes, and they want one last brilliance before the summer ends, and who better to give it them than she? The pen has underlined* brilliance *twice, and there is a blot where she changed her mind about the form and then changed it back.)*
> "Well," Rose says aloud, to no one, which is how she has conducted most of her important correspondence for ten seasons. She takes out her own paper — the Park's, plain, the watermark a small oak — and answers in the hand Quill once called "the only honest thing in Chancery." The reply is short. It says yes before it says anything else. It says the Park will open its doors and its cellars and its ballroom and whatever else a final brilliance requires. It says: come and plan it with me, and do not bring your modiste's nerves, only your ideas.
> "Lavinia's final," Letitia reads over her shoulder, and the hawk's eyes sharpen. "Darling, you have written* candles *in the glory column. Candles are cost."
> "For Lavinia's last, they are glory." Rose does not look up. "You taught me the difference. Glory is what the ton remembers. Cost is what the household book forgives."
> "The household book," Letitia says, sitting down with the air of a general opening a campaign, "forgives nothing. It merely waits." She uncaps her pen. "But for Lavinia —" the pen pauses — "for Lavinia, it will wait beautifully. Now. The musicians. How many, and how loud, and whether the young will dance until the candles gutter, because they will."
> *(The planning begins then, in the library, over chocolate going cold: the musicians, the flowers, the supper, the form of the thing still undecided and therefore still perfect. Outside, the Park does its morning work — the gardeners at the beds, the grooms at the yard — unaware that inside the house, at the library desk, the last brilliance of the Season is being built, one column at a time.)*

### L11.S2 · [D] · Rose, Lavinia
*Purpose: Lavinia arrives — the glittering, the planning; the loyal's, the final's. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The drive at eleven, and a carriage coming at a speed that makes the head groom wince from the yard. Lavinia arrives the way weather arrives — all at once, and with opinions. She is out of the carriage before the steps are properly down, her maid following with three hatboxes and the air of a woman who has given up arguing, and she crosses the gravel with her arms already open.)*
> "Darling!" The embrace smells of orange-flower water and the London road. "The fête! I have had seventeen ideas since dawn and rejected eleven of them, which leaves six, which is —" she holds up her fingers, counting — "too many. Rose. Darling. We must plan it *properly*. A final brilliance is not thrown, it is —" she searches the air — "it is *composed*. Like music. Like war."
> *(They walk up the steps arm in arm, and Lavinia talks the whole way — the young are restless, she says, the Season has been thin, the last weeks before summer want something to remember — and Rose listens the way she has learned to listen to Lavinia: for the heartbeat under the glitter. It comes as they cross the hall, sudden and quiet:)*
> "They were good to me, you know." Lavinia's voice drops, and the glitter goes with it, just for a breath. "The young. When I was —" she does not finish. "This is for them, really. For the house." She squeezes Rose's arm. "For you." Then the sparkle is back, bright as a struck match. "Now. The form, darling. I have been *agonizing*. A breakfast? No — too early, the young are never awake. A rout? Too crowded, one loses the — the —" she gestures — "the *shine* of it."
> "A ball," Rose says.
> "A ball." Lavinia stops dead in the middle of the hall, and the footmen go around them the way water goes around a stone. "Yes. Darling, yes. The night, the candles, the floor. The young want the night — they always have." She takes both of Rose's hands. "We will give it them, darling. The last brilliance. Glittering. Loyal." A pause, and the real voice again, quick as a slipped stitch: "Final."
> *(They stand there a moment, hands joined, in the hall of the finished house — the elder at twenty-five and the younger at twenty-four, though no one who saw them would guess which was which — and the planning of Lavinia's final fête moves from the library's columns into the air between them, where all of Lavinia's best plans have always lived.)*

### L11.S3 · [D] · Rose, Lavinia
*Purpose: the plan — the final brilliance; the fête's form, imagined. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The morning room, given over to the campaign. Lavinia has commandeered the long table, the map of the Park's state rooms, and all of Letitia's pencils, and she paces before it like a commander who has mislaid her army but remembers exactly where she left it.)*
> "The ballroom, obviously." She slaps the plan. "Forty paces, darling, I measured it in my head on the drive down and I am *never* wrong about floors. The supper in the dining room, the cards in the —" she waves — "the small saloon, the one with the green curtains. And the terrace for the intervals, because the young will want air and the night will want them."
> "The music?" Rose asks, taking notes in the margin of the plan.
> "Twelve." Lavinia does not hesitate. "No — fourteen. Darling, one cannot economize on *fiddles*. The young dance until the candles gutter, Letitia says, and she is right, they do, I have *seen* them." She stops pacing. "The flowers. All Hartwell-grown — the gardener will weep with pride, I have decided he will. And the supper —" she closes her eyes — "the supper must be the kind they talk about in October. The ices. The — the —" she opens her eyes — "Rose. *Ices in the shape of roses.* Can the cook do it? Tell me the cook can do it."
> "The cook," Rose says carefully, "can do anything except be hurried."
> "Then we shall not hurry her. We shall *worship* her." Lavinia drops into a chair, suddenly serious — the glitter thinning, the heartbeat showing through. "Darling. Listen." She leans forward. "I want it perfect. Not grand — perfect. The young will remember this night when they are old and married and dull, and I want them to remember —" she stops. "I want them to remember it was *kind*. That someone gave them a last brilliance and asked nothing for it." The glitter returns like a curtain dropping. "Also the ices. The ices are *essential*."
> *(Rose writes* ices, rose-shaped *in the household book, underlining it twice the way Lavinia underlines everything, and the fête takes its form on the page: the ballroom, the terrace, the fiddles, the Hartwell flowers, the supper they will talk about in October. The plan is Lavinia's. The ink is Rose's. Between them, the final brilliance is imagined whole.)*

### L11.S4 · [T] · Rose, Lavinia
*Purpose: the young — Lavinia's six, the twelve; the advance guard, the restless. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The saloon at four, and Lavinia's young arriving in a clatter of hooves and laughter — the advance guard of the fête, come down early to be useful and staying to be delightful. Lavinia's own circle at the core, and more orbiting, and they fill the saloon the way swallows fill a sky: all motion, all noise, all grace.)*
> *(Rose watches from the doorway a moment before going in, and what she sees is something other than the restlessness Lavinia described — a kind of held breath. They are young in the particular way of people who have survived a war's ending and do not quite know what the peace is for. They perch on the furniture their mothers would forbid, they argue about the ball's supper with the gravity of diplomats, and when Lavinia sweeps in they rise like a wave.)*
> "My dears!" Lavinia opens her arms to the room. "My *advance guard*. You are early, you are loud, and you are *exactly* what the fête requires. Now —" she claps — "the ballroom must be walked. The floor must be judged. I need opinions on candlelight, and I need them *honest*."
> *(They follow her out in a flock, and Rose follows the flock, and the ballroom receives its first inspection: the young pace the forty paces, test the floor with their slippers, stand where the musicians will stand and declare the acoustics — one of them, a girl with a laugh like a bell, actually sings a scale — and pronounce the room, by acclamation, perfect.)*
> "It wants," says a young man with more hair than sense, standing in the center of the floor with his arms spread, "it wants *us* in it."
> "It shall have you," Lavinia promises, "and it shall have the candles, and the fiddles, and ices in the shape of roses." A cheer goes up that the housekeeper will hear in the still-room and smile at. "But first —" Lavinia's voice drops into conspiracy — "first you must swear. The fête is *secret* until the invitations go. Not a word in London. Not a whisper."
> *(They swear it, hands stacked in the middle of the ballroom like conspirators, and the oath dissolves immediately into laughter, and the laughter into a reel danced without music on the perfect floor. Rose stands at the ballroom door and watches the advance guard take possession of the night before it happens, and understands what Lavinia meant about loyalty: it is not given to houses. It is given to people, and then the houses get it too.)*

### L11.S5 · [T] · Rose, Lavinia
*Purpose: the preparations — glittering; the Park, the brilliant's. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The Park, three days before the fête, and the whole house tilted toward the preparations like a ship under full sail. Rose walks the grounds with the household book and finds the brilliance being built everywhere she looks — the gardeners cutting for the ballroom, the footmen carrying chairs to the terrace, the still-room a battlefield of preserves for the ices.)*
> *(In the ballroom, the chandeliers come down for cleaning, and the maids polish each crystal with the tenderness of nurses. The candles arrive from London in crates marked* FRAGILE *in a hand that suggests the sender did not believe it. Letitia receives them in the hall with the air of an admiral taking on powder, and counts every one — twice — while the footman waits with the respectful patience of a man who has learned that the quartermaster's arithmetic is not to be hurried.)*
> "Every candle," Letitia announces, closing the household book on the count. "The ballroom, the terrace, the stairs. Darling, if the ton complains of the light, they are complaining of the *sun*."
> *(In the kitchens, the cook has been visited by Lavinia — a state visit, with presents — and has agreed to the rose ices on condition of absolute sovereignty over the menu. "The young," the cook tells Rose, serene among her coppers, "will eat what is put before them, miss, and be grateful, miss. The ices are the crown. The rest is the —" she searches — "the setting." Rose does not argue with sovereignty.)*
> *(On the terrace, the gardeners arrange the tubs of orange trees, and the evening rehearses itself: the stone still warm from the day, the Park lying green beyond, the first stars considering whether to come out. Rose stands at the terrace edge and does the arithmetic Letitia taught her — once for the sum, once for the nerve — and finds the sum is large and the nerve is steady. Every room is being polished, provisioned, or counted. The Park is ready to glitter.)*
> *(She writes it in the household book that night, in the column Letitia calls glory: the ballroom, the terrace, the fiddles, the candles, the rose ices. Underneath, in smaller hand, she adds what the book cannot hold: that the house has never looked more itself than it does tonight, three days from a party, humming like a hive.)*

### L11.S6 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the gowns — the young's; brilliance, the dressed. (Turns: 7)*
> *(Her bedroom, the afternoon of the fête, and the gowns laid out on the bed like a hand of cards — Lavinia's doing, of course, who arrived with her maid and three bandboxes and the firm conviction that Rose could not be trusted to choose alone. "Darling," she had said, "you dress for *battles*. This is a *brilliance*. Different theatre entirely.")*
> *(Rose stands before the bed in her petticoat and regards the options the way she regards everything: as a problem of logistics. The blush silk — Lavinia's choice, scattered with the small bright embroidery that catches candlelight. The deep blue — severe and true, the color of a night that keeps its promises. The ivory — plain as a vow.)*
> "The young," Lavinia says, draped over the chaise with a fashion plate she is not reading, "will wear *everything*. Muslins like foam. Silks like armor. One of them —" she lowers her voice — "one of them told me her gown cost more than her brother's horse. Darling, the *gowns* are the fête's first brilliance. Before the candles. Before the fiddles."
> "And what will you wear?" Rose asks.
> Lavinia sits up. "The silver." She says it the way other women say a prayer. "The gown, not the hair, darling, I am *twenty-four*. It has waited long enough for a night worthy of it." The glitter thins, just for a breath. "This is the night. My last brilliance. It should see me —" she stops — "as I was. As I am." The curtain drops back. "Also it makes my eyes look *enormous*."
> *(They dress together, the way sisters do, with the maid running between them and the pins flying. Lavinia laces Rose and Rose hooks Lavinia, and the bedroom fills with the particular intimacy of women preparing for a night that matters — the shared mirror, the borrowed pearl, the honest verdict.)*
> "There," Lavinia says at last, stepping back. "Darling. *There*." In the glass, Rose sees the gown she chose and the woman the house chose, and they are — for tonight — the same person. "You are dressed," Lavinia says softly, "and the night had better look sharp."

### L11.S7 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the music — the young's; the brilliant's sound. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The ballroom at seven, empty before the guests, and the musicians arriving with their instruments like a small invading army — fourteen of them, led by a first violin with the bearing of a field marshal and a cello player who keeps apologizing to his own instrument. They tune in the gallery, and the sound falls into the empty ballroom like rain into a well.)*
> *(Rose stands in the middle of the forty paces and listens. The tuning resolves — the field marshal taps his bow — and the first violin sings a phrase alone, testing the room. The ballroom answers: the plaster, the glass, the waxed floor give the note back warmer than it came. The cello player stops apologizing and smiles.)*
> "The air," says the first violin, coming down to the floor with his bow still raised, "the air in this room, madam —" he bows to Rose — "it *carries*. We shall not have to force it. The room does half our work."
> "The room," Rose tells him, "has had ten seasons of practice at carrying things."
> *(They rehearse the opening set — a quadrille, then a waltz, then the country dances the young will demand when the night is old — and the ballroom fills with it: the fiddles bright as cut glass, the cello underneath like a heartbeat, the flutes threading through like light through leaves. In the kitchens below, the cook's staff moves faster without knowing why. On the stairs, a footman pauses with a tray and listens.)*
> *(Lavinia arrives mid-rehearsal, already dressed, the silver gown catching the unlit chandeliers' ghosts. She stands beside Rose and does not speak — for Lavinia, an extraordinary discipline — until the waltz ends.)*
> "Darling," she says then, very quietly, the real voice, no glitter at all. "That is the sound the Season has been missing." She takes Rose's hand. "Thank you for letting me —" she stops. "For the *fiddles*, darling. Fourteen of them. You are *magnificent*." The glitter returns, bright as the violins. "Now. The candles must be lit at half past eight *precisely*. I have told Letitia, but tell her again — the quartermaster respects *repetition*."

### L11.S8 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the supper — glittering; the young's feast. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The kitchens at eight, and the supper in its final staging — the cook's sovereignty on full display. Rose descends, as she always does before a great night, to pay her respects to the true power of the house, and finds the kitchen in the ordered frenzy of a well-run war: the coppers steaming, the pastry cooks flour-white as ghosts, the footmen lined up for the trays like troops for inspection.)*
> "Miss Hartwell." The cook receives her among the steam with the serenity of a commander who has already won. "The supper." She gestures, and the gesture takes in the whole gleaming length of preparation: the raised pies, the jellies trembling in their molds, the cold fowls, the fruits, and at the center — on a bed of ice, in state — the ices. Roses, in ice — blush-pink, perfect, melting even as they are admired.
> "They are," Rose says, "impossible."
> "They are," the cook agrees, "the crown." She lowers her voice. "The young will eat them first, miss, before the soup if we let them. I have instructed the footmen —" a glance at the line — "the ices go out *with* the champagne, not after. A brilliance must be *timed*, miss."
> *(Rose tastes nothing — the cook's rule, never taste before the guests — but she walks the length of the tables and does the other arithmetic, the one Letitia taught her: enough for the expected, and enough again for the unexpected, because the young bring friends the way the tide brings shells. There is enough. There is more than enough — the tables are laid, the trays are lined up, the night is fed before it begins.)*
> *(On her way up, she meets Letitia on the stairs, carrying the last of the seating cards.)*
> "The supper," Letitia says, "smells like victory." She fans herself with the cards. "Darling, I have seated the dragon's —" she catches herself — "I have seated *everyone* where they will be happiest, which is to say where they will cause the least trouble. The young are together. The old are together. The dangerous are —" she smiles the quartermaster's smile — "separated by at least two chairs and one footman."
> *(They climb together toward the light and the music beginning above, and the house smells of wax and orange-flower and roasting fowl, and the fête — three days planned, a lifetime imagined — is ready.)*

### L11.S9 · ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — THE BRILLIANCE'S FORM (the fête's form: a breakfast, a rout, or a ball). The day deepens; decide the form. (Turns: 3 — the decision)*
> *(The library at dusk, three days before the fête, and the brilliance asking its form. Rose stands at the window with Lavinia's letter in one hand and Letitia's household book in the other, and the day deepens around the question the way dusk deepens around the Park: gradually, then all at once.)*
> *(A breakfast: mornings, and glitter. She pictures it — the young arriving sleepy and laughing, the terrace in morning light, chocolate and rolls, the fête done by noon. It would be *charming*. It would also be over before the candles were ever lit, and Lavinia's brilliance — she knows this the way she knows her sums — was never a morning thing. Mornings are for planning. Lavinia does her glittering after dark.)*
> *(A rout: evenings, and noise. The crush, the whole ton pressed into the state rooms like flowers in a book. It would be *grand*. It would also be — she pictures her young lost in the crowd, Lavinia's toast drowned in the general roar — the opposite of loyal. A rout belongs to everyone. This fête belongs to *someone*: to Lavinia, to the young, to the last brilliance.)*
> *(A ball: night, and the final hours. The forty paces, the fiddles, the candles, the ices in the shape of roses going out with the champagne. The young dancing until the candles gutter. Lavinia in silver, at the center of it, glittering to the last. It would be grand — grander than a breakfast, grander than a rout — and grandeur has its own cost, measured in candles and nerve.)*
> *(Letitia comes in with the evening post and reads the indecision off Rose's face the way she reads everything: at a glance, and aloud.)*
> "Darling." The hawk's eyes, amused. "You are standing at a window holding my household book like a *novel*. Decide."
> "Breakfast wastes the candles," Rose says. "A rout wastes the *toast*. A ball —" she turns from the window — "a ball spends both, and asks the night to pay it back." She opens the book. "Write it down, whatever it is. The ink will decide when I have finished talking."
> "The ink," Letitia agrees, uncapping her pen, "is a coward. It only writes what you tell it." The pen hovers. "Well, darling?"
> *(The form is chosen — and the fête takes its shape in the household book: the hours, the dancing, the small hours of the morning. Outside, the Park darkens into its evening order, unaware that inside the library, the last brilliance has just been given its hours.)*
> *(The game remembers the choice — and the means: breakfast, rout, or ball.)*
*★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — The brilliance's form:*
- **A breakfast** — *the morning, glittering.* The fête is a breakfast — the morning's, the young's. (Consequence: the form is the morning's — but mornings are early, and the brilliance wants the night.)
- **A rout** — *the evening, brilliant.* The fête is a rout — the evening's, the glittering's. (Consequence: the form is the evening's — but routs are crowds, and the loyalty wants the intimate.)
- **A ball** — *the night, final.* The fête is a ball — the night's, the last brilliance. (Consequence: the form is the night's — but balls are grand, and the fête was the young's.)

### L11.S10 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the fête — the glittering, held; the final's, the night's. (Turns: 8)*
> *(Half past nine, and the fête held — the ballroom lit, the terrace open, the night arrived in its chosen form. > *(The guests come up the stairs in a stream — the young first, as Lavinia ordained, in their dozens, and then the ton behind them, curious, glittering, disarmed by the candles before they can decide to be critical. > *(The dancing begins with the quadrille, and the forty paces fill — the young precise in the figures, the older guests watching from the sides with the particular smile of people remembering their own quadrilles. She dances the second with Julian, who does not apologize for anything and dances like a man who has finally stopped counting his debts.)*

"Lavinia's final brilliance, held. *Held*, darling. Drink."
> *(Rose drinks, and the champagne is cold and the night is warm, and the fête goes on behind her, brilliant to the last.)*

### L11.S11 · [D] · Rose, Lavinia
*Purpose: Lavinia, the hostess — glittering, triumphant; the loyal's, the final's. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The ballroom at ten, and Lavinia in her element — the hostess triumphant, the silver gown moving through the candlelight like moonlight through water. She claims the room not by raising her voice but by lowering it: the guests lean in to hear her, and leaning in, they are hers.)*
> *(Rose watches from the side of the floor, between dances, and sees the hostess work the way she has seen generals work a map — a word here, a laugh there, a hand on an elbow steering the shy young man toward the wallflower girl, a glance that tells the musicians to play the waltz again because the floor is asking for it.)*
> "Darling!" Lavinia seizes Rose by both hands, breathless, radiant. "The fête! The —" she gestures at the glittering room — "the final brilliance, darling! The young are —" she stops, and the real voice slips through, quick and unguarded — "the young are *happy*, Rose. Look at them. Actually happy." The glitter returns like a tide. "The ices! Did you see? They ate them with the *champagne*, exactly as the cook commanded. Sovereignty *vindicated*."
> *(A young girl — one of the twelve, in muslin like foam — approaches, trembling, and curtsies to Lavinia with the gravity of an ambassador.)*
> "Miss Lavinia." The curtsy deepens. "I — we —" she glances at her friends, clustered behind her — "we wanted to say. The night. It is the —" she searches — "the most *brilliant* thing. Ever."
> *(Lavinia looks at the girl for a long moment, and the hostess's glitter goes very still — and then she bends, silver gown and all, and kisses the girl's cheek.)*
> "Darling." The real voice, steady this time, held for a full three heartbeats. "It is for *you*. All of it. Remember it when you are old and married and dull, and be —" she stops herself — "be *glittering* anyway." The glitter returns, bright as ever. "Now go and dance, darling. The waltz is *waiting*."
> *(The girl flies back to her friends, hand pressed to her cheek, and Lavinia turns to Rose with the hostess's smile pinned on like a brooch — but underneath it, for just a moment, Rose sees the true thing: the loyal, triumphant, spending herself like candlelight, and glad of the spending.)*

### L11.S12 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the young, the dancing — brilliance, the danced; the night's, the glittering's. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The waltz at eleven, and the young dancing — the brilliance in motion, the night turning. Rose stands at the ballroom's edge with Henry, who does not dance if he can help it, and watches the floor turn.)*
> *(They dance well, the young — better than their mothers did, Letitia says loudly to anyone listening, and no one contradicts her. The six lead, the twelve follow, the orbiting dozens find their places, and the forty paces fill with the particular geometry of a waltz danced by people who have practiced it in their dreams. The candlelight moves on the silks. The fiddles carry the turn. The floor — waxed, perfect — gives back every step.)*
> "They are," Henry says beside her, "very young."
> "You disapprove, Major?"
> "I envy them." He says it plainly, the soldier's honesty. "At their age I was —" he stops. "Elsewhere." The smallest smile. "It is good to see the night used for *this*. Dancing. Rather than —" he does not finish, but Rose hears the rest: rather than war.
