## Occasion
London, Sunday — three days after the masquerade. The pulpits' harvest: the sermons take up Rose as their text, and respectability becomes a weapon. The war's shape was spoken on the great house's steps at past one in the morning; the war is not over.

## What changes
- The sermons: the rector of St. James's preaches the daughters of Zion, and the town hears the application — the fête's excess, the masquerade's license, the modiste's girl. The same sermon makes Henry's garrison wreck its warning.
- Augusta takes the war to holy ground — the drawing-room campaign, scripture and social artillery, Letitia at her shoulder.
- The town's mood swings: the fête's goodwill spent — a canceled visit, a cold shoulder at church, a name left off a list.
- Rose's answer is not pulpit warfare: the village, the poor, the unglamorous charity — the warrior's discipline.
- The romances tested small and human: a club blackball threatened, a dinner uninvited — and answered.
- Bell prints the sermons, and withholds what she cannot prove.
- The atelier's first real wound: Élise's name spoken from a pulpit.
