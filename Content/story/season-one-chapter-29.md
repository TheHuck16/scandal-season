# Scandal Season — Season One, Chapter 29: "Dinner en Famille"

**Chapter 29 — "Dinner en Famille"**
**STATUS: FULL** — September 27, 2026.

**Occasion:** The night before the Last Assembly; the family dining room; the eve-of-battle supper. **Preparation ritual:** the family gown — a dinner dress for the people who made her, not the room that will judge her (~25 micro-decisions). **Purse shelf:** the table is set with the house's best; the purse rests — tonight is not for spending.

**What changes for Rose:** She sets the fourth chair and fills it; she takes counsel from each of her four and decides how to carry it; she receives the household's blessing in all its forms; and she faces the night — sleep, preparation, or letters — on her own terms.

**Sources:** `story/season-one-scenes.md` (Ch 29 inventory), `story/book-one-arc.md` (S1 engine — "The Codicil"), `content/chapter-dressing.md` (ritual rules), `content/characters.json` (canon names).

**Canon applied:** 1815. Mrs. Nance Bell is the Gazette's publicly named proprietor from the first edition — no authorship hunt anywhere; the paper judges, never a mystery. Suitors honorable; the routes grew through friendship.

### L29.S5 · [C] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the family-gown ritual — a dinner dress for the people who made her. Direction + occasion brief, ~25 micro-decisions, story morsels, reveal coda.*
> **Occasion brief (published):** "Dinner en famille on the eve of battle — the last private evening of the season. The dress code: belonging, not display; the gown must be one the Park would recognize. Nothing that strives; nothing the table has not seen before, in spirit. Mourning colors forbidden; gloves expected at table. **Editorial criterion: belonging.**"
> **The 3 directions:** *The Daughter of the House* (ease, heritage — the Hartwell girl at her own table) / *The Soldier's Supper* (quiet armor — correct, composed, ready) / *The Beloved* (softness, for the people at the table — let them see her unguarded).
> **Decomposed steps (~25, 5 coins each, ~125 coins):** direction + brief → dress silhouette → bodice cut → bodice fabric (muslin / cambric / soft merino) → bodice color → sleeve style → sleeve fabric → sleeve color → neckline → skirt style → skirt fabric → skirt color → trim → shoes → gloves → hair → hair piece → earrings → pendant → brooch (the family piece, if owned) → beauty → shawl → reticule → final pin → the mirror, approved.
> **Story morsels (between pins):** Augusta, unexpectedly — her girlhood: the de Valcourt table in Paris before the Revolution, a story she has never told; Letitia on Thomas — one line, then silence, the wound touched once and laid down; the cook's intelligence, via the kitchen maid — what the market says of Rackham's credit (thin, and thinning); the housekeeper — which servants have asked to follow Rose to the Park when it's hers again: loyalty, named, all of them; Hodges' letter, read aloud by Augusta — the roof, the rents, the arithmetic of survival.
> **Jewels (owned pieces only):** the jewel shelf is open at any time and never a ritual step; the ritual's jewel steps draw only on pieces the player already owns, entering as ordinary options with no scoring edge.

### L29.S11 · [D] · Rose, the guest
*Purpose: the guest's counsel — the eve-of-battle beat, per the fourth chair.*
> *(If the leading gentleman dines:)*
> *(— Julian, fire: "I'll be the one not sleeping." Strategy and intimacy, over the cleared plates — "Tomorrow, don't watch Rackham. Watch Drummond. Rackham's beaten; Drummond's *buying*. Then" — and here the caretaking, not the declaration — "eat something at breakfast. Promise me.")*

### L29.S13 · plot beat · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 2/3 — THE COUNSEL. How does she carry it into tomorrow?*
> *(Four counsels — fire, light, steadiness, calm. The eve is ending; the Assembly is coming. The question is not what they said. It is what she does with it.)*
*★ KEY DECISION 2/3 — The counsel:*

- **Question it** — *She tests each counsel against her own judgment; some she keeps, some she sets down.* "'Watch Drummond' — yes. The rest — I will decide in the room."
- **Set it aside** — *Tomorrow is hers alone; she thanks them and fights it her way.* "I love them for it. And I am going in alone."
