### L6.S1 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the morning room — daylight; the witnesses gather; the concession, about to be spoken. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The morning room at half past nine, and the light does exactly what it promised at dawn: it comes through the east windows in solid bars and lays itself across the Turkey carpet like a thing with weight. The chairs are set in their rows, as for the ball's receiving, only now nobody is moving. The footmen hold the doors. The whole of the ton that stayed the night is here — the old guard, the young, the émigré houses, the bishops — and the talk, which was loud in the breakfast room, has gone very thin and very careful.)*
> *(She stands at the room's center. The ivory gown is gone — changed at seven, before the household was properly awake, for the morning's dress, the deep blue with the level line at the hem — and her hands, which were steady all through the night, have gone and turned honest at last: they want to shake. She lets them. Nobody is looking at her hands.)*
> *(Augusta is already seated, the spectacles on, seventy-two years of patience arranged in a chair. Letitia is by the windows, not sitting — the quartermaster never sits before a reckoning — turning her bracelet round and round her wrist. Honoria stands near the door, pale, the only Drummond in the room so far, and keeps smoothing a skirt that does not need smoothing.)*
> "Miss Hartwell." *(The footman, at the door, and the voice of him lands like a hammer on glass.)* "Lady Agatha Drummond."
> *(The room turns. Drummond enters — the morning's dress, deep purple again, because of course it is purple, the dragon exact in the doorway — and crosses to the room's center, where Rose waits. The two women face each other in full daylight, before the ton she tried to keep away, and the morning room holds its breath.)*
> *(She takes the room in as she has taken a hundred rooms: the chairs, the faces, the light. The bishops sit together, grave as rooks. The émigré houses cluster near the windows, already half-composing. Old Lady Micklethwaite has her lorgnette out, which means she intends to miss nothing and forgive everything. The footmen at the doors stand like sentries at a treaty signing, which is, Rose supposes, exactly what this is.)*
> *(Her heart is doing something unseemly under the morning's blue. Twenty-five, and the mistress of Hartwell Park, and her heart is hammering like a girl's. She lets it. The hands may shake and the heart may hammer; the voice, when it comes, will be level. It always is.)*

### L6.S2 · [D] · Rose, Lady Agatha Drummond
*Purpose: the acknowledgment — spoken in daylight; the house, conceded. (Turns: 10)*
> *(Drummond stops three paces from Rose. She does not look at the witnesses. She looks at Rose, and only at Rose — the same look she must have given her own mirror at dawn, rehearsing.)*
> "Miss Hartwell." *(The exact voice, the daylight's, no flourishes left in it.)* "Last night I attended your ball." *(A pause. The morning room's silence has a texture now, like stretched linen.)* "I saw the house — the ballroom, the garden, the bloom. I saw the ton — come, despite my efforts." *(Another pause, longer, the hardest kind.)* "I saw what was built."
> *(Rose says nothing. There is nothing to say yet, and she has learned, over ten seasons of mornings like this one, that the silence after a concession's first words is doing half the work.)*
> "Hartwell Park is magnificent." *(Drummond's eyes do not waver.)* "The house. The Park. The —" *(the word arrives, costed, paid for)* "— the achievement. I acknowledge it. Before the company." *(A breath, taken visibly, like a swimmer's before the cold plunge.)* "The war is over, Miss Hartwell. Genuinely."
> *(The words land in the room as the dawn's bars of light landed on the carpet: with weight, and everywhere at once. Somebody in the back — a young one, one of Lavinia's six — lets out a breath that turns half into a laugh before it is swallowed. Augusta does not move. The spectacles have gone very still.)*
> *(Rose hears, underneath the silence, the small honest arithmetic she always hears: the letters that will not now be written, the debts that will not now be called, the guests who will not now be warned away from her rooms. It is a long list. She lets it run its course and then sets it aside, because the list is not the moment.)*
> "Lady Drummond." *(Her own voice, level, the morning's.)* "The house thanks you. And I —" *(the direct look, the equal's)* "— I accept the acknowledgment. Genuinely."
> *(The hardest word hangs in the air — achievement — and Rose watches the room hear it. Augusta's spectacles have gone very still indeed. Letitia's bracelet has stopped. Honoria, by the door, has both hands pressed flat to her skirt, as though holding herself upright by main force.)*
> *(Drummond stands in the bars of light, the purple magnificent, and waits. The dragon does not fidget. The dragon has said the thing, and the thing is said, and now the room must do with it what rooms do.)*

### L6.S3 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the ton, witnessing — the old guard, the young; the concession, seen. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The silence breaks. It does not break as applause breaks — nobody is so coarse as to clap — but in the particular sound of four hundred years of breeding trying to absorb a historical fact: the rustle, the shifted chair, the murmur that starts in the old guard's corner and spreads like tea through hot water.)*
> *(The old guard nods. Old Lady Micklethwaite nods first, the enormous nod of a woman who has been waiting to be proved wrong and is enjoying it, and the rest follow — the Grevilles, the Sunderlands, the bishops — the slow grave nodding of people who have seen everything and have just seen one more thing.)*
> *(The young stare. Lavinia's six stare openly, mouths in various stages of the unattainable, and one of them — the youngest, all elbows and brilliance — whispers something to her neighbor that makes the neighbor's eyes go round. They will talk about this for years. They will tell their daughters about it, inaccurately, and the inaccuracy will become the legend, and the legend will be better than the truth, and Rose does not mind. Legends are a kind of interest, and interest compounds.)*
> *(The émigré houses vibrate — the French murmur, the quick hands, the Parisian assessment of a scene's staging — and somewhere among them Laurent is smiling the particular smile of a man who has watched the English do something magnificent and is already composing the letter home.)*
> *(They will write it tonight: the letters, the journals, the talk. The dragon conceded in daylight, before the ton, the house acknowledged. The morning room, half past nine. The carpet's bars of light. What was built, conceded — and the ton, the whole ton, saw it.)*

The sharp eyes say: I am counting the chairs. The mouth says: I am enjoying this enormously. The notebook says: the verdict, in the making.)*
> *(The murmur rises and falls, rises and falls, as the sea does against shingle. History, being recorded in real time, sounds remarkably like a tea party.)*

### L6.S4 · [D] · Rose, Augusta
*Purpose: the Dowager, watching — Augusta sees the dragon concede; the old enemies, the peace. (Turns: 8)*
> *(Afterward, when the room has emptied its breath and the witnesses have begun their careful drifting toward the doors — the old enemies do not rush — Augusta beckons. She has not risen. Seventy-two, and she has earned the right to receive her granddaughter sitting down.)*
> "Child." *(She takes Rose's hand in both of hers. The skin is thin and spotted and warm.)* "I have waited —" *(the voice does the extraordinary thing it does, the catch that is not weakness)* "— I have waited a very long time. Agatha Drummond, acknowledging. In daylight. Before the ton." *(The spectacles lower. The eyes behind them are bright in a way Rose has seen exactly twice before: once at the portrait's unveiling, once the night the house's roof went on.)* "The old guard, child, has seen everything now. There is nothing left to show them. We may —" *(the rarest smile)* "— we may rest."
> "Are you satisfied, Grandmama?" *(Rose keeps her voice low. The room is still full of people being careful.)*
> "Satisfied?" *(Augusta turns the word over as she turns everything: a jeweler with a stone.)* "Child, I am at peace." *(She squeezes the hand.)* "The war's over. The house stands. The line continues —" *(a glance toward the windows, the Park beyond, the daylight)* "— in stone, in flowers, in the woman." *(She holds the hand a moment longer than courtesy requires.)* "You did what I could not, child. I fought her with letters and pride, and she outlasted me. You fought her with the house, and the house —" *(the voice, steady)* "— the house did not need to outlast anyone. It simply stood."
> *(Rose bends and kisses the spotted hand. The Dowager's peace, witnessed: the oldest eyes in the room, satisfied at last.)*
> *(The room drifts on around them — the witnesses finding their groups, the talk resuming its careful pitch — and Augusta holds Rose's hand a moment longer, the spotted hand warm.)*
> "Seventy-two." *(Augusta says it suddenly, the spectacles direct.)* "I have been waiting since I was forty, child. Thirty-two years of Agatha Drummond." *(The rarest smile.)* "I shall spend the peace being magnificent at other people. It will be a novel sensation."
> *(Rose laughs — the first true laugh of the morning — and the Dowager's eyes shine, and the morning room holds them both in its bars of light.)*

### L6.S5 · [T] · Rose, Letitia
*Purpose: the quartermaster, witnessing — Letitia sees the peace; the maps, history. (Turns: 7)*
> *(Letitia is at the windows still, where she was before it happened, and she has not moved since. The bracelet has stopped going round her wrist. Her eyes — the hawk's eyes, wet — are fixed on the place where Drummond stood, as though the words might still be hanging in the air there, visible, to be checked once more for forgeries.)*
> "Darling." *(She turns, and the laugh comes out wet and entirely unplanned, like all her best sounds.)* "She said it. In daylight. Before everyone. The exact words, darling — magnificent, the achievement, genuinely. I have had those words described to me in seventeen different letters over ten seasons, and every one of them was a wish. This one —" *(she gestures at the morning room, the emptying chairs, the carpet's bars of light)* "— this one happened."
> *(She takes Rose's hands, and her own are cold, which is how Rose knows the morning cost her more than she will ever admit. The quartermaster's hands are never cold. They were cold at the creditors' meetings and cold at the blackballing and cold every time the letters came, and they are cold now, at the peace.)*
> "The maps, Aunt." *(Rose says it gently, because somebody has to.)*
> "The maps." *(Letitia looks toward the library, through the walls, as she always has.)* "They go in the library. Framed. Every chair, every letter, every debt called or forgiven — the whole war, mapped, dated, annotated." *(The wet laugh again.)* "Harrow can catalogue them. He will enjoy it far too much. He will use his smallest handwriting." *(She squeezes the hands.)* "We did it, darling. You did it. The quartermaster merely —" *(the hawk's eyes shine)* "— the quartermaster merely kept the ammunition dry."
> *(The strategist, at rest: the peace, witnessed. Letitia Harrow, who mapped a war, stands in the morning's light and lets the maps become history.)*
> *(The morning room is emptying now — the witnesses off to their letters, the chairs being righted — and Letitia stands at the windows watching the drive, where the first carriages are already being brought round. The hawk's eyes are dry now. The wet was for the moment. The quartermaster does not waste ammunition on the aftermath.)*
> "Seventeen letters." *(She says it suddenly, half to herself.)* "I counted, darling, on the way in. Seventeen times I was told those exact words in a letter, and every one was somebody's hope." *(She turns, the real smile.)* "Eighteen's the charm."

### L6.S7 · [D] · Rose, Lady Agatha Drummond
*Purpose: Rose's terms — the reconciliation's terms, stated; equals, in daylight. (Turns: 9)*
> *(The morning room empties slowly, as great rooms do after great moments — nobody wanting to be the first to break the spell, everybody wanting to be the first to write the letter. > "Lady Drummond." *(Rose says it with the exact courtesy, and means it.)* "The war is over. Genuinely." *(She looks at the dragon — fifty-four, exact, the purple magnificent in the morning light — and says the thing she decided, at dawn, watching the light come up over the lake, that she would say.)* "The terms are these. No humiliation — then, or now. The house acknowledged —" *(a breath)* "— and the peace, kept. Not as victor and vanquished." *(The direct look. The equal's.)* "As equals."
> *(Drummond turns the word over as Augusta turns words: the jeweler, the stone.)*
> "Equals." *(A pause. The morning room's silence is different now — the after-silence, the held breath let out.)* "Miss Hartwell —" *(the rarest thing: the formality, set aside, deliberately)* "— Rose. ### L6.S9 · ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — THE CONCESSION'S SHAPE (how Rose receives the acknowledgment: graciously, formally, or warmly). The morning deepens; decide the receiving. (Turns: 3 — the decision)*
> *(The morning deepens — the witnesses dispersing to their letters, the house settling into its peace — and the question of the receiving waits: the acknowledgment was spoken, the terms were stated, the hand was taken. > *(Graciously: the winner's ease, the magnanimity that costs nothing and shows everything. Formally: the equal's exactness, the courtesy returned in the coin it was paid in. Warmly: the future's openness, the door left ajar for what the peace might become.)*
*★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — The concession's shape:*
- **Graciously** — *the winner's ease.* Rose receives it with magnanimity; the war's winner, at ease. (Consequence: the ease is absolute — but ease is distance, and the peace wants nearness.)
- **Formally** — *the equal's exactness.* Rose receives it with exact courtesy; equals, the terms kept. (Consequence: the exactness is perfect — but formality is armor, and the daylight wants the woman.)
- **Warmly** — *the future's openness.* Rose receives it with warmth; the peace, embraced. (Consequence: the warmth is real — but warmth is risk, and the dragon is still the dragon.)
> *(The game remembers the choice — and the means: graciously, formally, or warmly. The receiving, held in the manner she chose, becomes the peace's first habit.)*
> *(Graciously would be the magnanimous thing, and she is magnanimous as the house is magnificent: by construction. Formally would be the exact thing, and exactness has served her ten seasons. Warmly would be the brave thing, and she has been brave before breakfast.)*
> *(The morning room's light has moved westward. The witnesses have gone to their letters. The peace waits for its first habit, and the habit will be hers to set.)*

### L6.S12 · [T] · Rose, Lady Agatha Drummond
