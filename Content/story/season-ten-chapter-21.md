### L21.S1 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the years' turning — the free life's years; Julian, the free. (Turns: 7)*
> *(She wakes before the house, which is unlike her — the household book usually gets her first — and goes to the terrace in her slippers because the morning has the particular blue of a day that intends to be fine. The stone is cold through the soles. The damp patches from the night are drying in order, dark to pale, like ink blots in reverse.)*
> *(The terrace is empty. It will not be, later — Julian walks it before she wakes now, the way other men read the paper — but the air still holds him, the way rooms hold people who have just left them. She lays her hand on the balustrade and finds the stone cold. Not his warmth. The night's.)*
> *(Below, the Park wakes in its order: the blackbird first, then the mist lifting off the hollows, then the gardener's boy with the barrow, whistling through his teeth. She watches the light come down the balustrade in a line, touching each stone in turn, and thinks about the turning of the years — not as an event, with trumpets, but as this: the light, arriving, stone by stone.)*
> *(This terrace has seen him in shadow. She does not need to name the year; the stone remembers the grip of his hand on nights when the coercion still had him by the throat. The shadow is gone from it now. What walks here in the mornings is a man of twenty-eight with his gloves in his pocket and no one's permission required.)*
> *(She thinks about the free life's years the way she thinks about everything: as an account. On one side, the shadow years — the debts, the fear, the brother's name unspoken. On the other, this: the terrace, the light, the man. The book balances. It has never balanced so quietly.)*
> *(The mist is off the hollows now. The gardener's boy has stopped whistling and started working, which is the Park's version of applause. Somewhere below, a door closes — the house beginning its day — and she knows that in an hour Julian will come up from the garden with earth on his boots and the morning in his face, and the terrace will be his again, the way it is every morning.)*
> *(The sun reaches the top of the balustrade. She takes her hand away — the stone keeps the cold a moment longer, then gives it up — and goes in to order the chocolate and open the book. The years have turned. The terrace is his. The day, like the stone, is warming.)*

### L21.S2 · [D] · Rose, Julian
*Purpose: Julian, the years' — the free man, the free; the unguarded, the kept. (Turns: 8)*
> *(The breakfast room, and Julian is late — unheard of, these years — and comes in with his village coat on and his hair still damp from the pump, and pats his breast pocket twice before he sits down, the way he does. The chocolate is already poured. Rose watches the patting and says nothing. She has learned that the pocket holds its own counsel.)*
> *(He eats. He reads the Gazette's second page and snorts at it. And then, between the toast and the marmalade, he reaches into the pocket — not the pat, the reaching — and lays a folded paper on the tablecloth beside her plate. It is soft with handling, the creases gone white.)*
> *"The last of them," he says.*
> *(Rose unfolds it. A receipt, in a tradesman's hand: paid in full. The date is a fortnight past. She looks at the sum — he watches her look — and folds it again before she can do the arithmetic aloud, which is his form of mercy.)*
> *"Paid," Julian says. "All of them. Every scattered debt my brother left across three counties, paid — and before you open your mouth, Rose, with money I earned, which I am saying aloud so you cannot offer yours again. You offered in the second year. You offered in the fifth. The answer is still no, and it is still final."*
> *"I wasn't going to offer," Rose says.*
> *"You were doing the arithmetic. I could see it. The household-book look." He takes the receipt back and folds it smaller. "The sum is between me and the ironmonger. What you may know is the date, and the fact, and that I have been carrying it for a fortnight, showing it to no one, patting it like a love letter. I have been a man with a secret, and the secret was solvency."*
> *(She laughs — and the breakfast room holds the sound, chocolate and marmalade and all — and understands what the paper actually is. Not a receipt. A discharge. Ten years of his brother's debts, and the coercion they bought, and the fear that came with it — all of it, settled, quietly, in coin he earned himself.)*
> *"What does a man do," Julian asks, "with a finished arithmetic?"*
> *"He frames it," Rose says, "or he forgets it."*
> *(He considers. Then he opens the drawer of the breakfast table — the one that holds the spare spoons and the string — and lays the receipt inside, among the string. "For the mice," he says, and closes the drawer, and asks for more toast. The drawer holds its paper. The room holds its morning. The debts hold nothing any more.)*

### L21.S3 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the mornings — Julian's; the free's, the kept. (Turns: 7)*

### L21.S4 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the debts — the paid; the years', the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The paying was quiet. That is what Rose comes back to, turning it over in the study with the household book closed before her — because she never recorded his debts, not one of them; he would not permit it, and she would not have known how to enter them. The coercion's arithmetic was never hers to keep.)*
> *(She had insisted on coming for the last of it. The ironmonger's shop in the town, the bell over the door, the smell of oil and metal and coal dust. The ironmonger — a man with ink on his cuffs and thirty years of other people's debts in his book — had looked up, and recognized Julian, and reached for the ledger without being asked.)*
> *(The entry was there, in the old hand: the brother's account, scattered across the years, the small sums that had kept a younger son in chains. Julian had counted out the coin on the counter — his coin, earned, every piece of it — and the ironmonger had counted it back, and drawn a line through the entry with a steady pen, and closed the book. No scene. No witnesses but her, and the shop cat asleep on the nails.)*
> *"That," the ironmonger had said, "is the last of it." And Julian had nodded, and they had stood a moment in the shop with the bell still trembling over the door, and then they had walked out into the daylight with nothing following them.*
> *(Rose thinks about the machinery — the plot arc's phrase, the one Julian uses himself now: the machinery that makes younger sons into instruments. The debts were never about money. Money was the handle. The paying broke the handle — quietly, at a counter, in daylight. The sums were incidental.)*

> *(She opens the household book, and writes nothing — because there is nothing to enter, and that is the point — and closes it again. The study holds its quiet. Somewhere below, Julian is laughing at something the dog has done. The line holds. The debts hold nothing.)*

### L21.S5 · [D] · Rose, Julian
*Purpose: Julian, the debts' — the free man, the paid; the gloves, the kept. (Turns: 8)*
> *(Evening, the morning room, and the fire is doing its low work, and Julian takes the gloves from his pocket — not the pat, the taking — and lays them on the table between them the way other men lay down cards. The mending shows in the firelight: the seam at the thumb, the stitching uneven, determined, his.)*
> *(Rose picks one up. The leather is soft with years. The stitches wander — up, down, up again, like a drunk walking home.)*
> *"Yours?" she says.*
> *"Mine. The second mending." He holds out his hands: the fingers, the pricked places. "A fortnight of evenings with the housekeeper's mending basket. She supervised. She despaired. She has stopped speaking to me about it, which I take as approval."*
> *"The stitches wander."*
> *"The stitches are free. They go where they like." He takes the glove back, gently, the way other men handle letters. "They were mended in poverty, Rose. The first time — you remember the first time. Necessity, and no money for new. They are kept by choice now. There is a difference, and I intend to be particular about it."*
> *(She watches his face in the firelight — the unguarded look, the years' own — and understands that the gloves are not gloves. They are the sign. The free man's sign, kept by choice, mended by his own hand, the stitches wandering where they like.)*
> *"Show me," she says, and he takes up the housekeeper's needle — she has left it, despairing, on the mantel — and a handkerchief, and demonstrates the stitch with his tongue between his teeth, and pricks his finger, and sucks it with enormous dignity.*
> *"The free man," Rose says, "bleeds onto his own handkerchief."*
> *"The free man," Julian says, around the finger, "mends what he keeps."*
> *(She tries one on — her hand smaller, the glove loose at the fingers — and holds it up to the firelight, the mended seam showing.)*
> *"I remember the first mending," Rose says. "The shadow years. You wore them anyway, to everything, because there was nothing else."*
> *"I wore them," Julian says. "They wore me. It was a mutual arrangement." He takes the glove back — gently, the way other men handle letters — and folds the pair, the mended seam inward. "The poverty's mending was hidden. This one is done by the fire, in the open. That is the difference, and I intend to be particular about it."*
> *(The fire does its low work. The gloves go into the pocket. The pat. And Rose understands the keeping for what it is: the free man choosing, daily, what the poor man could only endure.)*

### L21.S6 · [T] · Rose (alone)
*Purpose: the evenings — Julian's; the free's, the kept. (Turns: 7)*
> *(The inn at the crossroads, and the monthly whist, and Julian goes openly — which is the thing, Rose thinks, watching him take off his coat in the inn's low room: once he would have avoided the county's eyes, taken the edges, the shadows. Now he walks in through the door like a man entering his own house.)*
> *(The room smells of ale and pipe smoke and the fire. The squire's wife deals. The cards go round. Julian plays badly — spectacularly, cheerfully badly — and loses the first rubber with the air of a man who has discovered a new talent for it.)*
> *"Strategy," he says, when the squire's wife takes the trick. "A deep strategy. Imperceptible. Even to me."*
> *(The room laughs — the easy laugh of people who have decided to like a man — and Rose, in the corner with her tea, watches the free man among the county: the laugh, the ease, the hand played wrong and owned at once. No edges. No shadows. The inn's fire on his face, and the face unguarded.)*
> *(He loses half a crown. His own money — earned, counted out in daylight at the ironmonger's counter a fortnight past — lost cheerfully across a card table. He tries to win it back. He loses more. > *"Another?" the squire asks.*
> *"Another," Julian says. "The strategy requires time. Like varnish. Like debt, in reverse."*

### L21.S10 · [D] · Rose, Julian
