### L20.S5 · plot beat · Rose Hartwell
*Purpose: ★ KEY DECISION 1/3 — THE SERVANTS' HALL. Reward the household's loyalty openly, quietly, or with the Park's future.*
> Monday afternoon. Mrs. Pye's letter lay open on the desk, and Rose did the arithmetic no ledger could do for her: the price of loyalty, and the manner of its paying.
> *(System: three ways to reward the hall. The household hid the war's traces — Jem's refused crown, the maids' silence, the kitchen's double feeding. The reward must match the service; the manner will be remembered below stairs for a generation.)*
> Reward them openly: a gift for every servant, given by her own hand in the hall, named and thanked before them all. The honor would be public, the gratitude unmistakable — and the talk, inevitably, would travel: a household rewarded on the eve of the assembly invites the town to ask *what for*.
> Reward them quietly: the gifts distributed by Mrs. Pye, with Rose's thanks carried privately — no assembly of the hall, no speeches, no audience. The dignity of discretion — and the risk that quiet gratitude reads, below stairs, as the gratitude of those who are embarrassed to be grateful.
> Promise the Park's future: no gifts now, but a promise — restoration, security, a place in the Park's future for every servant who stood the war. The grandest reward, and the cheapest in the present — a promise is only as good as the assembly, and the hall knows it.
> Rose looked at Mrs. Pye's last line — *or I shall be cross, and you know what that means* — and smiled, and chose the way she had chosen everything that season: exactly, and in the knowledge that the manner would outlive the money.
> *(System: the choice is Rose's. The hall will remember the manner for a generation.)*
