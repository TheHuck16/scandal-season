## 5. Prose law (from prose-standards.md — the brief fails if these appear)

- Human-clean from the first draft. Concrete period detail; varied sentence length; wry human dialogue (Letitia is funny without trying; Octavia is precise; Bell is blunt; Élise is warm). Rose thinks in logistics and ledgers; she feels in images she would never say aloud.
- NEVER: not-X-but-Y reversals ("it wasn't grief, it was arithmetic"); the mechanical `’s whole X` / `'s whole X` possessive tic (the S6 disease: thousands of thoughtless occurrences — the occasional deliberate S1-style use like "the Season's whole arithmetic" is fine); abstraction triplets; thesis dialogue (people deflect, joke, change the subject); moral-summary closers (end on image/gesture/dialogue); doubled restatements (turn line advances, never echoes).
- Em-dashes: structural separators (`— remembered:`, option `**Label** —`, turn lines) don't count. In narrative prose, don't use em-dash parentheticals as a crutch — most sentences get none. Measurable check: `grep -v '^> (T[0-9]' FILE | grep -v '— remembered:' | grep -v '^- \*\*' | grep -o '—' | wc -l` should stay under 350 per chapter (S1's approved chapters sit near 330).
- NEVER the tics: "she writes it in the book", "remembered her" (as a phrase), "in the series".
- 350–450 words of prose per ordinary scene; the ritual runs long, outside the budget.
- Vary turn-line constructions — don't stack possessives mechanically ("the letter: the old's, the claim's") scene after scene; S1's chapters use a fraction of that density.
- Banned words: delve, tapestry, vibrant, testament to, intricate, nestled, a symphony of.
